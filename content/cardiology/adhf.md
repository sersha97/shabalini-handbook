:::bottom
- **Profile the patient:** warm/cold × wet/dry. Most are **warm & wet → diurese**.
- **Flash pulmonary edema with high BP** → **BiPAP + high-dose nitroglycerin**, then diuretic.
- **IV loop dose = 1–2.5× the home oral dose** (DOSE). Furosemide-naive: 40–80 mg IV.
- **Check response at 2 h (spot urine Na >50–70) and 6 h (UOP >100–150 mL/h).** Weak response → **double the dose**, then add a thiazide or acetazolamide.
- **Cold & wet / shock** → ICU, inotrope; see cardiogenic shock.
- **Keep GDMT** unless shock or symptomatic hypotension. Start **SGLT2i** in hospital.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- HF = **high filling pressure** (congestion) ± **low output** (hypoperfusion).
- Most admissions are **fluid redistribution or accumulation** over days: high LV pressure → pulmonary venous pressure → **alveolar edema**.
- Low output → kidneys sense "low volume" → **RAAS + sympathetic + ADH** → more salt and water retention → a vicious circle.
- **Venous congestion** (high CVP) hurts the kidney more than low output → creatinine often **improves** with diuresis.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- Clinical: dyspnea, **orthopnea**, PND, edema, JVD, rales, S3.
- **BNP >100 / NT-proBNP >300** supports HF (lower in obesity; higher in CKD, AF, age).
- CXR: cephalization, Kerley B, effusions. **POCUS**: B-lines, plethoric IVC.
- TTE: EF, valves, RV, pericardium (new HF or change).

:::pearl
**Find the trigger — "FAILURE":** **F**orgot meds/salt · **A**rrhythmia (AF) / **A**nemia · **I**schemia/infection · **L**ifestyle (salt, alcohol) · **U**pregulation (pregnancy, thyroid) · **R**enal failure · **E**mbolism / **E**ndocarditis. Plus: NSAIDs, TZDs, non-DHP CCB, uncontrolled HTN, valve disease.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

ECG, troponin (flat mild elevation is common), BMP, Mg, CBC, BNP, TSH, iron studies (ferritin, TSAT), A1c, lipids, TTE. New cardiomyopathy → ischemic evaluation.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Diuresis
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| Start | Furosemide IV 1–2.5× home PO dose BID (or bolus + drip). Bumetanide 1 mg ≈ furosemide 40 mg IV |
| 2 h | Spot urine Na **<50–70 mEq/L** → poor response |
| 6 h | UOP **<100–150 mL/h** → poor response |
| Poor response | **Double** the loop dose (ceiling ~200–250 mg IV furosemide) |
| Still poor | Add **metolazone** 2.5–5 mg PO / chlorothiazide 500 mg IV, or **acetazolamide** 500 mg IV (ADVOR) |
| Refractory | Ultrafiltration / HF team |

- Goal: **euvolemia**, not a weight number. Net −1 to −3 L/day typical.
- Check BMP + Mg daily (K, Mg go down; Na can drop with thiazides).
- Creatinine rise ≤0.3–0.5 with good decongestion → **keep going** (permissive).
=== Hypertensive pulmonary edema
- **BiPAP** (or CPAP) right away.
- **Nitroglycerin**: SL 0.4–0.8 mg q5 min, then IV infusion starting ~50–100 mcg/min and titrate fast (high-dose strategy) to SBP ~140.
- Then IV loop diuretic. Many are **not** total-body overloaded — they are "volume-shifted."
- Avoid morphine (worse outcomes).
=== GDMT in hospital
- **Continue** β-blocker, ARNI/ACEi/ARB, MRA unless shock, symptomatic hypotension, severe AKI/hyperkalemia.
- **Hold β-blocker** in cardiogenic shock or new cold & wet profile.
- **Start SGLT2i** once stable (EMPULSE: better outcomes, works in HFrEF and HFpEF).
- **STRONG-HF:** rapid up-titration before and soon after discharge → fewer readmissions and deaths.
- IV iron if ferritin <100 or 100–299 with TSAT <20% (HFrEF).
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **Under-dosing** diuretics because "creatinine is up."
- Discharging still congested (weight ≠ euvolemia; check JVP, orthopnea).
- Stopping GDMT for asymptomatic soft BP.
- Missing hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia → arrhythmias.
- Fluid bolus to a "cold" HF patient without checking filling.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| SBP 210, RR 34, frothy sputum, SpO₂ 84% | BiPAP + high-dose nitrates |
| Cool extremities, Cr rising, SBP 85, rales | Cold & wet → inotrope (dobutamine/milrinone) |
| Poor UOP after 80 mg IV furosemide | Double the dose before adding a second agent |
| HFrEF admission, BP 110/70, K 4.2 | Continue GDMT; start SGLT2i |
| Diuretic resistance + contraction alkalosis | Acetazolamide |

## 🏠 Discharge checklist

- [ ] Euvolemic on exam; oral diuretic tested ≥24 h
- [ ] GDMT started/up-titrated (all 4 pillars in HFrEF)
- [ ] BMP within 1 week; **follow-up visit within 7 days**
- [ ] Daily weights; call if +2 lb/day or +5 lb/week
- [ ] Na ~2 g, avoid NSAIDs; vaccines; cardiac rehab referral (HFrEF)
