:::bottom
- **Unstable because of AF?** (shock, ischemia, pulmonary edema) → **synchronized cardioversion ≥200 J** now.
- **Find the fire.** Sepsis, bleed, hypovolemia, PE, pain, withdrawal, thyroid. Fast AF is often the *alarm*, not the fire.
- **Look for pre-excitation.** Very fast (>250), wide, changing QRS → **no AV nodal blockers**.
- **Know the EF.** EF >40% → metoprolol or diltiazem. EF ≤40% → **no diltiazem**; use amiodarone or digoxin.
- **Target** resting HR <110 if symptoms are controlled.
- **Decide on anticoagulation today** (CHA₂DS₂-VASc) — before any cardioversion, electrical *or* chemical.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Trigger + substrate.** Ectopic beats, mostly from the **pulmonary veins**, start AF. Stretched, scarred atria keep it going. AF remodels the atria → *AF begets AF*.
- **The AV node is the gatekeeper.** Atria fire 350–600/min. The AV node lets only some through. Rate-control drugs act on the AV node.
- **Why patients crash.** Fast rate shortens diastole → less LV filling and less coronary flow. Loss of the atrial kick hurts stiff ventricles most (HFpEF, AS, HCM).
- **Why strokes happen.** No organized atrial contraction → stasis in the **left atrial appendage** → thrombus → embolism.

## 🔎 Diagnosis & classification

{{ecg:af|Irregularly irregular R–R, no distinct P waves, fibrillatory baseline.}}

| Element | Criterion |
|---|---|
| ECG | Irregularly irregular R–R, no repeating P waves |
| Duration needed | Full 12-lead, or ≥30 s on a strip |
| "RVR" | Ventricular rate >100–110/min |

**2023 ACC/AHA stages:** 1 At risk → 2 Pre-AF → 3A Paroxysmal (≤7 d) · 3B Persistent (>7 d) · 3C Long-standing (>12 mo) · 3D after successful ablation → 4 Permanent.

| Mimic | Clue | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| MAT | ≥3 P-wave shapes, COPD | Treat the lungs. No cardioversion, no OAC for MAT |
| Flutter, variable block | Sawtooth in II, III, aVF | Same OAC rules; ablation often cures |
| Sinus tach + PACs | P before each QRS | Treat the cause |
| Pre-excited AF | Very fast, wide, bizarre QRS | AV nodal blockers → VF |

## 🧪 Workup — and why

| Test | What it changes |
|---|---|
| 12-lead ECG | Pre-excitation, ischemia, baseline QTc |
| BMP + Mg | Replete K >4, Mg >2; CrCl for DOAC dosing |
| TSH | Thyrotoxicosis → propranolol, treat thyroid |
| CBC | Bleeding source; OAC bleeding risk |
| Troponin | Only if chest pain / ischemic ECG — a flat bump is usually demand |
| TTE | EF decides the drug; mitral stenosis / mechanical valve → warfarin |
| TEE | Before cardioversion if AF ≥48 h and no 3 weeks of OAC |

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Unstable **because of** AF?
Y: Synchronized cardioversion ≥200 J. Start heparin, but do not delay the shock.
N: Go on.
Q: Is there a cause that *needs* the tachycardia? (sepsis, bleed, hypovolemia, PE)
Y: Treat the cause first. Rate drugs can crash the BP.
Q: Pre-excitation on ECG?
Y: Procainamide or ibutilide, or DCCV. No BB, CCB, digoxin, adenosine, IV amiodarone.
Q: What is the EF and BP?
Y: EF >40% + BP OK → metoprolol or diltiazem · EF ≤40% → amiodarone or digoxin · soft BP → esmolol, amiodarone, digoxin
Q: Anticoagulation + rate vs rhythm plan
:::

:::tabs
=== Rate control
| Drug | Dose | Best for | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metoprolol tartrate | 2.5–5 mg IV over 2 min q5 min (max 15 mg); PO 25–100 mg BID | Most patients, ACS, thyroid | Decompensated HF, hypotension |
| Diltiazem | 0.25 mg/kg IV (~15–20 mg) over 2 min; repeat 0.35 mg/kg; drip 5–15 mg/h | EF >40%, COPD | **EF ≤40%**, hypotension |
| Esmolol | 500 mcg/kg over 1 min → 50–300 mcg/kg/min | Borderline BP | Often needs ICU |
| Digoxin | 0.25 mg IV q2h, max 1.5 mg/24 h | HFrEF, soft BP | Slow onset; CKD; with amiodarone |
| Amiodarone | 150 mg IV/10 min → 1 mg/min ×6 h → 0.5 mg/min ×18 h | Shock, HFrEF | **Can cardiovert** → stroke risk |

Do not stack IV diltiazem + metoprolol + amiodarone in one hour. Go oral early.
=== Rhythm control
- Consider when: first symptomatic episode, HFrEF / suspected tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy, rate control fails, younger patient, early AF (EAST-AFNET 4).
- **Cardioversion rules:** AF ≥48 h or unknown → 3 weeks of OAC *or* TEE first. After any cardioversion → OAC ≥4 weeks.
- **Drugs:** amiodarone (structural disease OK) · flecainide/propafenone only without CAD/structural disease (+ AV nodal blocker) · dofetilide/sotalol need inpatient QTc monitoring.
- **Ablation:** first-line for selected symptomatic patients; improves outcomes in HFrEF (CASTLE-AF).
=== Anticoagulation
| CHA₂DS₂-VASc | Points |
|---|---|
| CHF / EF ≤40% | 1 |
| Hypertension | 1 |
| Age ≥75 | 2 |
| Diabetes | 1 |
| Stroke / TIA / embolism | 2 |
| Vascular disease | 1 |
| Age 65–74 | 1 |
| Female sex | 1 |

- **ACC/AHA 2023:** OAC if ≥2 (men) / ≥3 (women). ESC 2024 uses CHA₂DS₂-VA (no sex point): OAC if ≥2.
- **DOAC over warfarin** — except mechanical valve or moderate–severe mitral stenosis.
- Apixaban 5 mg BID; reduce to 2.5 mg if **2 of 3**: age ≥80, weight ≤60 kg, Cr ≥1.5.
- HAS-BLED finds fixable bleeding risks. Falls alone rarely justify no OAC. **Aspirin is not stroke prevention.**
- HCM + AF → anticoagulate regardless of score.
:::

:::dose
Doses are typical adult doses with normal kidney and liver function. Check the dose in CKD, liver disease, frailty or low weight.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **Shooting the fire alarm** — HR 135 in septic shock is compensatory. Diltiazem → hypotension.
- Forgetting that **amiodarone can cardiovert** → same clot risk as DCCV.
- **Diltiazem in HFrEF** — negative inotrope.
- Calling every troponin bump an NSTEMI.
- Not replacing K and Mg.
- No anticoagulation plan (or insurance check) at discharge.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem clue | Answer |
|---|---|
| AF, BP 78/40, confused, HR 170 | Synchronized cardioversion now |
| Irregular, >250/min, wide QRS changing shape | Pre-excited AF → procainamide / DCCV |
| AF + EF 30% — which rate drug to avoid? | Diltiazem / verapamil |
| AF + mechanical mitral valve | Warfarin, not DOAC |
| Unknown duration, wants cardioversion | 3 weeks OAC or TEE; OAC ≥4 weeks after |
| Regular narrow tachycardia at exactly 150 | Think 2:1 atrial flutter |
| Starting amiodarone in a patient on digoxin | Cut digoxin dose |
| Life-threatening bleed on dabigatran | Idarucizumab |

## 🏠 Discharge checklist

- [ ] Resting HR <110 on a stable oral regimen
- [ ] Trigger found and treated (infection, thyroid, alcohol, lytes)
- [ ] TTE done or scheduled
- [ ] OAC decided, started, covered by insurance; patient taught
- [ ] Cardiology/EP follow-up in 1–2 weeks; ablation discussed
- [ ] OSA screen, weight, BP, alcohol counseling
