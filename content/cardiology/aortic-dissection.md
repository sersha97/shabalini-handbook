:::bottom
- **Think of it:** sudden, maximal-at-onset **chest/back pain** + **pulse or BP difference** (>20 mmHg between arms) or **neuro deficit** or **new AR murmur** or hypotension.
- **CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis now.** Unstable → **bedside TEE** (or TTE for flap, effusion, AR) on the way to the OR.
- **Anti-impulse therapy:** **IV β-blocker first** (esmolol or labetalol) → **HR <60** → then vasodilator (nicardipine/nitroprusside) → **SBP <120** within the first hour, as fast as tolerated. **Opioids** for pain.
- **Type A** (ascending involved) → **emergency cardiac surgery.** Mortality rises every hour.
- **Type B** uncomplicated → ICU, medical therapy (± TEVAR later). **Complicated** (malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain/HTN, rapid growth) → **TEVAR**.
- **No anticoagulation, no antiplatelets, no thrombolytics** until dissection is ruled out.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

{{fig:aorta-types}}

- A tear in the **intima** lets blood split the **media** → a **false lumen** runs along the aorta.
- The force that drives it is **dP/dt** — how fast pressure rises with each beat. That is why we slow the **heart rate first**, then lower BP.
- Complications follow the anatomy: **pericardium** (tamponade), **aortic valve** (acute AR), **coronaries** (usually RCA → inferior STEMI), **carotids** (stroke), **subclavian** (pulse deficit), **spinal arteries** (paraplegia), **mesenteric/renal** (gut ischemia, AKI), **iliacs** (cold leg).
- **Acute aortic syndromes** also include **intramural hematoma** (IMH, bleeding in the wall without a flap) and **penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer** (PAU). Same urgency rules by location.

**Risk factors:** hypertension (most common) · **cocaine/meth** · bicuspid aortic valve · **Marfan, Loeys-Dietz, vascular Ehlers-Danlos, Turner** · known aneurysm · recent aortic surgery/cath · pregnancy (3rd trimester) · family history · giant cell arteritis · weightlifting · fluoroquinolones (association).

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| ADD risk score (1 point per category) | Examples |
|---|---|
| **High-risk conditions** | Marfan/connective tissue disease, family history, known aortic valve disease or aneurysm, recent aortic manipulation |
| **High-risk pain** | Abrupt, severe, **tearing/ripping** chest, back or abdominal pain |
| **High-risk exam** | **Pulse deficit / BP differential**, focal neuro deficit + pain, new AR murmur + pain, hypotension/shock |

- **Score 2–3** → go straight to **CTA**.
- **Score 0–1** → consider **D-dimer**; <500 ng/mL makes dissection unlikely (ADvISED). Positive D-dimer or high suspicion → CTA.

| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **CTA chest/abd/pelvis** | First line. Shows type, extent, branch involvement |
| **TEE** | Unstable patient, in ED/OR. Also shows AR, tamponade |
| **TTE** | Quick clues: flap in the root, pericardial effusion, AR. Cannot rule it out |
| **MRI** | Stable patients, follow-up, contrast allergy |
| **CXR** | Widened mediastinum in a minority — a **normal CXR does not rule out** |
| **ECG** | Normal or nonspecific in most; **inferior STEMI** if the RCA is involved |

:::warn
**STEMI + back pain + pulse deficit = dissection until proven otherwise.** Lysis or heparin can be fatal. Get a quick bedside echo or CTA before the cath lab.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

Two large-bore IVs · **type and crossmatch** · CBC, BMP, **lactate** (malperfusion), troponin, coags · ECG · **BP in both arms** · arterial line (right radial usually, unless the right subclavian is involved) · urine output · serial neuro and limb exams · call **cardiothoracic/vascular surgery** early.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Anti-impulse drugs
| Step | Drug | Typical adult dose |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Rate (HR <60) | **Esmolol** | 0.5–1 mg/kg IV load → 50–300 mcg/kg/min |
| | **Labetalol** | 20 mg IV → 20–80 mg q10 min, or 0.5–2 mg/min |
| | β-blocker contraindicated | **Diltiazem** 0.25 mg/kg IV or verapamil |
| 2. BP (SBP <120) | **Nicardipine** | 5 mg/h, ↑2.5 mg/h q5–15 min, max 15 |
| | **Nitroprusside** | 0.3–0.5 mcg/kg/min, titrate (max 10) |
| | **Clevidipine** | 1–2 mg/h, titrate |
| 3. Pain | **Fentanyl / morphine** | Pain drives catecholamines and BP |
=== Type A
- **Emergency surgery** (ascending ± arch replacement; valve resuspension or replacement).
- Hypotension → think **tamponade, severe AR, rupture, coronary dissection** → straight to the OR.
- **Tamponade:** surgery is the fix. If arrest is imminent and surgery is not available, **small controlled pericardial drainage** only to restore minimal perfusion — full drainage can cause rupture.
- Very elderly/comatose/major stroke → individual decision with surgery.
=== Type B
- **Uncomplicated** → ICU, anti-impulse therapy, serial imaging. TEVAR can be considered in selected patients with high-risk features.
- **Complicated** (malperfusion of gut/kidneys/legs/cord, rupture, expansion, refractory pain or HTN) → **TEVAR** preferred over open repair.
- Spinal cord protection during TEVAR: **lumbar drain** in high-risk cases; keep MAP up.
=== Long term
- BP **<130/80** (lower if tolerated) and HR control — **β-blocker** backbone (± ARB).
- Imaging (CT/MRI) at ~1, 6 and 12 months, then yearly.
- Avoid heavy isometric lifting, stimulants. Treat smoking, lipids.
- **Genetic testing** and first-degree relative screening in young patients or family history.
:::

:::dose
Typical adult doses. Never start a vasodilator before the heart rate is controlled — reflex tachycardia raises dP/dt.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Thrombolysis or heparin for "STEMI" or "stroke" that is really a dissection.
- Vasodilator first → reflex tachycardia → tear propagates.
- Trusting a normal CXR or a normal D-dimer in a **high-risk** patient.
- Measuring BP only in the arm with the pulse deficit (falsely low → undertreatment).
- Missing malperfusion: check **lactate, legs, belly, urine, neuro** every hour early on.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Tearing pain, BP 180/100 right arm, 140/80 left | Aortic dissection → CTA |
| Tall young man, lens dislocation, chest pain | Marfan → dissection |
| Type A on CT, BP 90/50, muffled heart sounds | Tamponade → emergency surgery |
| Inferior STEMI + diastolic murmur + back pain | Type A involving RCA |
| Type B with rising lactate and abdominal pain | Mesenteric malperfusion → TEVAR |
| First drug for dissection, HR 110, SBP 180 | IV esmolol (or labetalol) |
| Type B, pain-free, stable on drugs | Medical therapy, ICU, serial imaging |
