:::bottom
- **Symptoms of severe AS** = **angina, syncope, dyspnea/HF** (or lower exercise tolerance). Once symptoms start, survival drops fast without valve replacement.
- **Severe AS:** **Vmax ≥4 m/s**, **mean gradient ≥40 mmHg**, **AVA ≤1.0 cm²** (AVAi ≤0.6). Discordant numbers → low-flow work-up.
- **Symptomatic severe AS → AVR (TAVR or SAVR)** — Class 1 in all guidelines. Heart Team decides the route.
- **Ward rules — "fixed door":** keep **preload** (gentle diuresis), keep **HR slow** and **sinus rhythm**, avoid **big vasodilator drops**; hypotension → **phenylephrine or norepinephrine**.
- Decompensated and too sick for AVR → **balloon aortic valvuloplasty** as a bridge.
- GI bleeding from angiodysplasia + AS = **Heyde syndrome** → AVR often stops it.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

{{fig:as-door}}

- Calcified (age, bicuspid valve) or rheumatic leaflets → **fixed obstruction** to outflow.
- LV adapts with **concentric hypertrophy** → stiff, **preload-dependent** ventricle that needs the **atrial kick**.
- **Angina:** thick muscle needs more O₂, while high LV pressure squeezes the coronaries and diastole is short.
- **Syncope:** on exertion, muscles vasodilate but cardiac output cannot rise through the narrow valve → BP falls.
- **HF:** high filling pressures (diastolic dysfunction), later the LV fails (low EF).

## 🔎 Diagnosis

**Exam:** harsh **crescendo–decrescendo** systolic murmur at the right upper sternal border → carotids · later peak = more severe · **soft or absent S2** · **pulsus parvus et tardus** (weak, late carotid upstroke) · S4.

| Stage | Vmax | Mean gradient | AVA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild | 2.0–2.9 m/s | <20 mmHg | >1.5 cm² |
| Moderate | 3.0–3.9 m/s | 20–39 mmHg | 1.0–1.5 cm² |
| **Severe** | **≥4.0 m/s** | **≥40 mmHg** | **≤1.0 cm²** |
| Very severe | ≥5.0 m/s | ≥60 mmHg | — |

:::tabs
=== Low-flow, low-gradient
AVA ≤1.0 cm² but gradient <40 mmHg. Is the valve really tight, or is the LV too weak to open it?

| Type | Next test |
|---|---|
| **Low EF (<50%)** — "classical" | **Low-dose dobutamine echo**: gradient rises ≥40 with AVA staying ≤1.0 → true severe AS. AVA opens up → **pseudo-severe** |
| **Normal EF, SVi <35 mL/m²** — "paradoxical" (small, thick LV; often HTN, women, AF) | **CT aortic valve calcium score**: severe likely ≥2000 AU men / ≥1200 AU women; very likely ≥3000 / ≥1600 |
=== Mimics
- **HCM (LVOT obstruction):** murmur **louder with Valsalva/standing**, softer with squatting; normal carotid upstroke (brisk, bifid).
- **Aortic sclerosis:** murmur without gradient (Vmax <2.5 m/s).
- **Mitral regurgitation:** holosystolic at the apex → axilla.
- **Subvalvular/supravalvular stenosis** (young patients) → echo.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

- **TTE** (severity, EF, other valves). Repeat if symptoms change.
- **ECG** (LVH, AF, conduction disease), **BNP** (prognosis), CBC (Heyde).
- **Cardiac CT (TAVR protocol)** and **coronary evaluation** (cath or CT) before AVR.
- **Exercise testing** only in **truly asymptomatic** patients — never in symptomatic severe AS.
- Consider **ATTR cardiac amyloid** in older patients with low-flow AS (common overlap) → PYP scan + light chains.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Ward & ICU tips
| Problem | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume overload** | Gentle loop diuretic, daily weights | Over-diuresis → preload crash |
| **Hypotension / sepsis** | Fluids in small boluses + **phenylephrine or norepinephrine** | Dobutamine/milrinone alone (vasodilate) — unless low-EF AS under expert care |
| **AF with RVR** | **Early cardioversion** if unstable; rate control carefully | Large IV diltiazem pushes in a hypotensive patient |
| **Hypertension** | Start low, go slow (ACEi/ARB OK) | Big drops with nitro/hydralazine drips |
| **Sedation / intubation** | Etomidate or small doses, pressor ready | Large propofol bolus |
| **Neuraxial anesthesia** | Discuss with anesthesia | Rapid sympathectomy |
=== Valve replacement
- **AVR indicated (Class 1):** **symptomatic severe AS** · asymptomatic severe AS with **EF <50%** · severe AS having other cardiac surgery.
- **Reasonable:** very severe AS (Vmax ≥5), abnormal exercise test, rapid progression, high BNP — in low-risk patients.
- **TAVR vs SAVR** (Heart Team + shared decision):
  - ACC/AHA 2020: **>80 y → TAVR**, **65–80 → either**, **<65 → SAVR** (anatomy, life expectancy, bicuspid valve matter).
  - **ESC/EACTS 2025:** **TAVI for age ≥70** with a tricuspid valve and suitable anatomy, whatever the surgical risk.
- **Balloon valvuloplasty:** bridge in shock or before urgent noncardiac surgery.
- **Palliative care** when AVR is not in the patient's goals or life expectancy is <1 year.
=== Asymptomatic severe AS
- **EARLY TAVR** (2024): early TAVR vs surveillance in asymptomatic severe AS → fewer deaths, strokes or unplanned CV hospitalizations (driven by hospitalizations); many in the surveillance arm needed AVR within ~2 years.
- **ESC/EACTS 2025** now supports **early intervention** in selected asymptomatic patients with severe high-gradient AS and normal EF if procedural risk is low.
- **US:** the 2020 ACC/AHA guideline predates EARLY TAVR. CMS (2026) still covers TAVR for asymptomatic AS only within approved studies. Expect change — check for a US update.
- Either way: **ask about symptoms carefully** — many "asymptomatic" patients have quietly slowed down.
=== Before noncardiac surgery
- **Symptomatic severe AS + elective surgery** → AVR (often TAVR) **first**.
- **Asymptomatic severe AS** → elevated-risk elective surgery is reasonable **with invasive monitoring**, careful volume, avoid tachycardia and hypotension.
- Urgent surgery → proceed with anesthesia/cardiology plan; BAV in selected cases.
:::

:::pearl
**No drug slows AS.** Statins did not work (SEAS, ASTRONOMER, SALTIRE) — treat lipids for atherosclerosis, not for the valve.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Calling it "flow murmur" in an older patient with syncope.
- Aggressive diuresis or nitro drip → sudden hypotension and arrest.
- Missing **low-flow, low-gradient** severe AS because "the gradient is only 30."
- Treating GI bleeding again and again without thinking of **Heyde syndrome**.
- Missing **ATTR amyloid** alongside AS in elderly patients.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 78-year-old, exertional syncope, late-peaking murmur, soft S2 | Severe AS → TTE → AVR |
| Severe AS, EF 25%, gradient 25, AVA 0.8 | Low-flow AS → dobutamine echo |
| Murmur louder with Valsalva, quieter with squatting | HCM (not AS) |
| AS + recurrent GI bleeding from angiodysplasia | Heyde syndrome (acquired vWF deficiency) → AVR |
| Severe AS patient becomes hypotensive after sedation | Phenylephrine / norepinephrine |
| 84-year-old with symptomatic severe AS, suitable anatomy | TAVR |
| 55-year-old, bicuspid valve, symptomatic severe AS, low risk | SAVR |
