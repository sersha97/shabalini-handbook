:::bottom
- **Symptoms + bradycardia?** (hypotension, AMS, chest pain, HF, syncope) → **atropine 1 mg** IV q3–5 min (max 3 mg).
- Atropine fails → **transcutaneous pacing** or **dopamine / epinephrine** drip → call cardiology for **transvenous pacing**.
- **Mobitz II and 3rd-degree block** are below the node → atropine often fails → plan to pace.
- **Look for reversible causes:** drugs (BB, CCB, digoxin), **hyperkalemia**, ischemia (inferior MI), hypothyroid, hypoxia, Lyme.
- Asymptomatic sinus brady or 1st-degree block → usually no treatment.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

{{fig:block-levels}}

- **Sinus node** problems → sinus brady, pauses, tachy-brady (sick sinus).
- **AV node** problems (above the His) → 1st degree, **Mobitz I**. Usually benign, often vagal or drugs. Narrow QRS escape ~40–60.
- **Infranodal** (His-Purkinje) problems → **Mobitz II**, high-grade, **3rd degree** with wide escape (~20–40). Unstable, can go to asystole.
- **Inferior MI** (RCA) → AV nodal block, often transient. **Anterior MI** → infranodal block, bad sign.

## 🔎 Diagnosis — the strips

{{ecg:avb1|1st degree: PR >200 ms, every P conducts.}}

{{ecg:mobitz1|Mobitz I: PR grows, then one P is not conducted. Groups of beats.}}

{{ecg:mobitz2|Mobitz II: PR fixed, sudden dropped beat. Often BBB.}}

{{ecg:chb|3rd degree: atria and ventricles beat independently (AV dissociation), P rate > QRS rate.}}

:::pearl
**2:1 block** cannot be called Mobitz I or II from one strip. Narrow QRS + improves with atropine/exercise → nodal. Wide QRS → assume infranodal.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

ECG · **BMP (K!)** · Mg · troponin · TSH · **digoxin level** · med list · Lyme serology (young, endemic area) · TTE · consider sleep apnea (nocturnal brady). Infiltrative disease (sarcoid, amyloid) if young or unexplained high-grade block → cardiac MRI.

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Symptoms or poor perfusion?
Y: Atropine 1 mg IV q3–5 min, max 3 mg.
N: Monitor, find cause, hold nodal blockers.
Q: Atropine fails?
Y: Transcutaneous pacing (sedate) or dopamine 5–20 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 2–10 mcg/min.
Q: Reversible cause?
Y: Treat it (see tabs). Pace temporarily if needed.
N: Mobitz II / high-grade / 3rd degree / symptomatic sick sinus → permanent pacemaker.
:::

:::tabs
=== Drug toxicity
| Drug | Antidote |
|---|---|
| β-blocker | Glucagon 3–5 mg IV, high-dose insulin (euglycemic) |
| CCB | Calcium (gluconate 3 g / chloride 1 g), high-dose insulin 1 U/kg bolus |
| Digoxin | Digoxin-specific Fab (DigiFab) |
| Organophosphates | Atropine (large doses) |
=== Hyperkalemia
Bradycardia + wide QRS → **calcium first**, then shift K. See hyperkalemia page. "BRASH" = Bradycardia, Renal failure, AV-nodal blocker, Shock, Hyperkalemia.
=== Ischemia
Inferior MI + block → usually resolves; atropine OK; pace if unstable. Anterior MI + new Mobitz II/3rd → transvenous pacing early.
=== Pacing tips
- **Transcutaneous:** pads anterior–posterior, rate 60–70, increase mA until **capture** (wide QRS after each spike) + **pulse** (check femoral). Needs sedation/analgesia.
- Atropine does **not** work after heart transplant (denervated).
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Treating the number, not the patient (athletes, sleep).
- Missing **hyperkalemia** or **digoxin** as the cause.
- Calling 2:1 block "Mobitz I" and sending the patient home.
- Pacing spikes without capture on the monitor.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic Mobitz I in a sleeping young man | No treatment |
| Mobitz II, asymptomatic, no reversible cause | Permanent pacemaker |
| CHB + target rash + facial palsy | Lyme carditis → IV ceftriaxone, temporary pacing |
| Brady + hypotension on verapamil overdose | Calcium + high-dose insulin |
| Brady, peaked T waves, CKD on lisinopril + BB | BRASH → calcium, treat K |
| Young patient, CHB, hilar adenopathy | Cardiac sarcoidosis |
