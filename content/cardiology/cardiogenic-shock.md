:::bottom
- **Cardiogenic shock** = low cardiac output + hypoperfusion (lactate ↑, cold, confused, oliguria) — BP may still be "OK" early.
- **Call the shock team early.** Transfer to a center with cath lab + mechanical support.
- **AMI-shock → emergent cath, culprit-only PCI** (CULPRIT-SHOCK).
- **Norepinephrine first** for MAP ≥65, then **inotrope** (dobutamine or milrinone) for low output.
- **Stop β-blockers**, negative inotropes and nitrates. Treat arrhythmias aggressively.
- Microaxial flow pump (Impella CP) improved survival in **STEMI-shock** (DanGer Shock); routine VA-ECMO did not (ECLS-SHOCK).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Pump fails → low CO → low BP → low coronary perfusion → **worse pump** (death spiral).
- Body responds with vasoconstriction (cold extremities) → higher afterload → even lower CO.
- Lactate rises, organs fail. **Every hour matters.**
- Causes: **AMI (~40–80%)**, acute-on-chronic HF, myocarditis, stress cardiomyopathy, valve rupture, VSR, arrhythmia, drug toxicity, RV infarct.

## 🔎 Diagnosis — SCAI stages

| Stage | Picture |
|---|---|
| **A** At risk | Large MI, HF — normal perfusion |
| **B** Beginning | Hypotension or tachycardia, **no** hypoperfusion |
| **C** Classic | Hypoperfusion → needs 1 pressor/inotrope or MCS |
| **D** Deteriorating | Worse despite initial therapy |
| **E** Extremis | Cardiac arrest / CPR / ECMO, refractory |

Hemodynamics: **CI <2.2 L/min/m²**, PCWP >15, SBP <90 for ≥30 min (or pressors). **Cardiac power output** <0.6 W = high mortality.

## 🧪 Workup

ECG (STEMI?) · troponin · **lactate q2–4h** · BMP, LFTs (shock liver), CBC · **bedside echo** (EF, RV, MR, VSR, tamponade) · ABG · **PA catheter** to guide therapy (CI, PCWP, RA, PAPi for RV).

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: STEMI or likely ACS?
Y: Cath lab now. Culprit-only PCI. Consider Impella CP in STEMI-shock (no RV failure).
Q: MAP <65?
Y: Norepinephrine.
Q: Low output despite MAP (cold, lactate ↑, low CI)?
Y: Add dobutamine 2.5–10 mcg/kg/min or milrinone 0.125–0.5 mcg/kg/min (CKD → avoid milrinone, causes hypotension).
Q: Still deteriorating (SCAI D–E)?
Y: Mechanical support (Impella, IABP, VA-ECMO) per shock team; consider advanced HF therapies.
:::

| Situation | Tip |
|---|---|
| **RV infarct / RV failure** | Careful fluids (CVP 10–15), avoid nitrates, inotrope, pulmonary vasodilators; RV support device |
| **Acute severe MR / VSR** | Afterload reduction + IABP → surgery |
| **Takotsubo with LVOT obstruction** | No inotropes; fluids, β-blocker, phenylephrine |
| **Beta-blocker/CCB overdose** | Calcium, high-dose insulin, glucagon |

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Waiting for hypotension — lactate and exam show shock first.
- Dobutamine alone in a hypotensive patient → vasodilation → BP drops.
- Multivessel PCI in shock (CULPRIT-SHOCK: worse).
- Missing tamponade or mechanical complications on echo.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Inferior STEMI, hypotension, clear lungs, JVD | RV infarct → fluids, avoid nitrates |
| Day 4 post-MI, new holosystolic murmur, shock | Papillary muscle rupture or VSR → echo, surgery |
| Apical ballooning, LVOT gradient, hypotension | Avoid inotropes |
| Cardiogenic shock, best first pressor | Norepinephrine |
