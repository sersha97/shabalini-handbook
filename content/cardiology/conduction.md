:::bottom
- **Path:** SA node → atria → **AV node** (delay = PR) → **His** → **right bundle** + **left bundle** (anterior + posterior fascicle) → Purkinje.
- **QRS ≥120 ms + typical shape = complete BBB.** 110–119 ms = incomplete BBB or nonspecific IVCD.
- **New LBBB + chest pain is not automatically a STEMI.** Use **Smith-modified Sgarbossa** + the clinical picture. Shock or acute HF → cath lab.
- **New RBBB + dyspnea or hypoxia → think PE.**
- **Bifascicular block + syncope** → admit on telemetry; risk of intermittent complete heart block → EP study / pacemaker if HV ≥70 ms.
- **Alternating BBB** (RBBB one beat, LBBB another) → **permanent pacemaker.**
- **LBBB + EF ≤35% + HF symptoms** on GDMT → refer for **CRT** (best if QRS ≥150 ms).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

{{fig:conduction-heart}}

- The **AV node** is the only normal electrical bridge between atria and ventricles. It conducts **slowly on purpose** so the atria finish filling the ventricles.
- Below the node, the **His–Purkinje** system is a fast highway. It activates both ventricles almost together → **narrow QRS (<120 ms)**.
- When a bundle is blocked, that ventricle fills late by **slow muscle-to-muscle spread** → **wide QRS** and a typical shape.
- The **right bundle** is a thin cord → easy to injure (RV strain, PE, a catheter in the RV). The **left bundle** is a wide fan → LBBB usually means **real structural disease**.
- **Blood supply:** AV node ← **RCA** (~90%). His and proximal bundles ← AV nodal artery + LAD septal branches. RBB and LAF ← **LAD**. LPF has a **dual supply** → isolated LPFB is rare and means wide disease.

:::analogy
Think of a building's power. The **AV node** is the breaker box (slow, safe, can be tripped by drugs and vagal tone). The **bundles** are the main cables in the wall. A tripped breaker can reset; a cut cable usually cannot — that is why **infranodal block needs a pacemaker**.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{fig:bbb-qrs}}

| Block | ECG criteria (adults) |
|---|---|
| **RBBB** | QRS ≥120 ms · **rsr′, rsR′ or rSR′ in V1–V2** · **wide S in I and V6** (longer than R or >40 ms) · normal R-peak time in V5–V6 |
| **Incomplete RBBB** | Same shape, QRS 110–119 ms (common, often normal) |
| **LBBB** | QRS ≥120 ms · **broad notched/slurred R in I, aVL, V5–V6** · **no septal q** in I, V5–V6 · R-peak time >60 ms in V5–V6 · **ST–T opposite (discordant) to QRS** |
| **"Strict" (Strauss) LBBB** | QRS ≥140 ms (men) / ≥130 ms (women) + QS or rS in V1–V2 + mid-QRS notching in ≥2 of I, aVL, V1, V2, V5, V6 → best CRT responders |
| **LAFB** | QRS <120 ms · axis **−45° to −90°** · qR in aVL · rS in II, III, aVF |
| **LPFB** | QRS <120 ms · axis **+90° to +180°** · rS in I and aVL · qR in III and aVF · exclude RVH, lateral MI, thin body habitus |
| **Bifascicular** | RBBB + LAFB (common) or RBBB + LPFB (high risk) |
| **Nonspecific IVCD** | QRS >110 ms, no BBB shape — think drugs, hyperkalemia, cardiomyopathy |

:::pearl
"**Trifascicular block**" is an old, confusing term. Write what you see: "bifascicular block + 1st-degree AV block." A long PR here can be from the node **or** from slow His–Purkinje conduction — the surface ECG cannot tell.
:::

### LBBB (or paced rhythm) and ischemia

In LBBB, the ST segment normally goes **opposite** the QRS. Look for what breaks that rule:

| Smith-modified Sgarbossa — any one = positive | |
|---|---|
| **Concordant ST elevation** ≥1 mm in any lead with a positive QRS | Very specific |
| **Concordant ST depression** ≥1 mm in V1–V3 | Posterior MI |
| **Discordant ST elevation** with **ST/S ratio ≤ −0.25** (STE ≥25% of the S-wave depth) | Replaces the old "≥5 mm" rule |

- Works for **ventricular-paced rhythm** too.
- Negative Sgarbossa does **not** rule out ACI. Trend troponin, compare old ECGs, get an echo for new wall-motion change.
- **Hemodynamic instability or acute HF + suspected ACS → urgent angiography**, whatever the ECG.

## 🧪 Workup

- **Compare with old ECGs** — new vs old changes everything.
- **BMP (K)**, Mg. Wide QRS can be **hyperkalemia** or **Na-channel blockers** (TCA, flecainide, propafenone, cocaine, diphenhydramine).
- **New LBBB** → **TTE** (cardiomyopathy, valve disease). Consider ischemic evaluation.
- **New RBBB** → think **PE**, RV strain, ASD, myocarditis; often benign if isolated and asymptomatic.
- **Syncope + BBB** → telemetry; **ambulatory monitor / loop recorder** if outpatient; **EP study** if still unexplained (measures the **HV interval**).
- Young patient with conduction disease → think **sarcoid, Lyme, myotonic dystrophy, lamin A/C**, amyloid (older) → cardiac MRI / genetics.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== By block
| Finding | What to do |
|---|---|
| Isolated RBBB or LAFB, no symptoms | Nothing. Document. |
| New LBBB | TTE; ischemia evaluation by clinical picture |
| Bifascicular block, no symptoms | No pacemaker. Follow up. |
| Bifascicular block + syncope | Monitor → EP study or ILR → **pace if HV ≥70 ms or infranodal block** |
| **Alternating BBB** | **Permanent pacemaker** (Class 1) |
| Mobitz II / CHB with wide QRS | Pacemaker — see Bradycardia chapter |
=== CRT & pacing
- **CRT (biventricular pacing):** EF ≤35%, NYHA II–IV on GDMT, sinus rhythm, **LBBB with QRS ≥150 ms** → Class 1. LBBB 120–149 ms or non-LBBB ≥150 ms → reasonable.
- **Conduction system pacing** (His bundle or **left bundle branch area pacing**) can correct some LBBB and avoids RV-pacing dyssynchrony. Now a guideline option (2023 HRS/APHRS/LAHRS).
- Expected **>20–40% RV pacing** with EF 36–50% → consider physiologic pacing to prevent pacing-induced cardiomyopathy.
=== Procedures that cause block
- **Right heart cath / PA catheter in a patient with LBBB** → the catheter can bump the RBB → **complete heart block**. Have pads on.
- **TAVR:** new LBBB is common afterward; **pre-existing RBBB** = highest risk of CHB → telemetry, low threshold to pace.
- **Septal alcohol ablation (HCM), aortic valve surgery, aortic root abscess (endocarditis)** → new block. New PR prolongation in aortic-valve IE = **abscess until proven otherwise**.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Calling every new LBBB a STEMI → unnecessary cath; or ignoring a **positive Sgarbossa** → missed occlusion MI.
- Missing **hyperkalemia** or a **Na-channel blocker** behind a "new IVCD."
- Floating a PA catheter in LBBB without pacing backup.
- Ordering a pacemaker for asymptomatic bifascicular block.
- Reading LVH or WPW as LBBB — look for the delta wave and short PR.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| rSR′ in V1, wide S in I and V6, QRS 130 | RBBB |
| Sudden dyspnea after surgery, new RBBB, S1Q3T3 | Pulmonary embolism |
| Axis −60°, qR in aVL, QRS 100 | Left anterior fascicular block |
| RBBB + LAFB, recurrent syncope, normal echo | EP study → pacemaker if HV ≥70 ms |
| RBBB on Monday, LBBB on Tuesday | Alternating BBB → permanent pacemaker |
| EF 25%, NYHA III on GDMT, LBBB QRS 160 | CRT-D |
| Aortic valve endocarditis, PR now 280 ms | Aortic root abscess → TEE, surgery |
| Chest pain, LBBB, 2 mm concordant STE in V5 | Occlusion MI → cath lab |
