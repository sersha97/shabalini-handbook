:::bottom
- **Know the device:** pacemaker or ICD? Leads or leadless? **Pacemaker-dependent?** Card, CXR, ECG, or the device clinic.
- **Magnet on a pacemaker → asynchronous pacing** (VOO/DOO). **Magnet on an ICD → shocks/ATP off, pacing unchanged.** Most **leadless** pacemakers ignore magnets.
- **One ICD shock, feels fine** → interrogation within days. **≥2 shocks, storm, syncope, chest pain** → ED/telemetry, **K/Mg**, ischemia check, **interrogate now**.
- **Inappropriate shocks** (AF, noise, lead fracture) → **magnet** to stop shocks while you get EP — on telemetry with **external defibrillator** ready.
- **Before cardioversion/defibrillation:** pads **≥8 cm from the generator** (anterior–posterior). **Interrogate after.**
- **End of life:** offer to **turn off ICD shocks** — a patient's right; it is not euthanasia.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Pacemaker:** senses the heart's own beats; if none comes in time, it **paces**. Leads in the RA and/or RV (or a capsule in the RV/RA for leadless).
- **ICD:** a pacemaker that also detects VT/VF → **ATP** (fast pacing to break VT painlessly) → **shock**. **S-ICD** (subcutaneous) shocks only, no routine pacing.
- **CRT:** a third lead (LV via coronary sinus) or conduction-system pacing → both ventricles squeeze together → better EF in LBBB.
- RV-only pacing activates the heart like **LBBB** → long-term can cause **pacing-induced cardiomyopathy** (high pacing burden).

## 🔎 Diagnosis — reading the device

{{fig:nbg-code}}

{{ecg:paced|Paced rhythm: a sharp spike, then a wide QRS (RV pacing looks like LBBB).}}

| Problem | ECG | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Failure to capture** | Spike, **no QRS/P after it** | Lead dislodgement (first weeks), high threshold (**hyperkalemia**, acidosis, ischemia, flecainide), lead fracture |
| **Failure to output** | Missing spikes when they should come | Oversensing (noise, EMI, T waves), battery depletion, lead fracture |
| **Undersensing** | Spikes **on top of** native beats, at random | Low signal (new MI, lead issue) — risk of spike on T → VF (rare) |
| **Pacemaker-mediated tachycardia** | Paced at upper rate limit, regular | Retrograde P triggers ventricular pacing loop → **magnet** stops it |

{{ecg:ftc|Failure to capture — check K, lead position (CXR), and call the device team.}}

:::pearl
A pacemaker is **not** a defibrillator. A patient with a pacemaker and VT/VF still needs ACLS. Do **CPR and shock** normally — just keep pads away from the generator.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

- **Interrogation** (device rep / EP clinic / remote monitoring): battery, lead impedance, thresholds, events, % pacing, stored VT/VF and shocks.
- **ECG** with and without magnet (pacemaker) — shows capture at the magnet rate and battery status (magnet rate drops when ERI).
- **CXR** (PA + lateral): lead position, fracture, pneumothorax (new implant).
- **BMP (K), Mg**, troponin (shocks), TSH, drug levels (antiarrhythmics, digoxin).
- **Device infection signs** (pocket redness, erosion, bacteremia, especially S. aureus) → blood cultures ×2–3, **TEE** → see Endocarditis.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== ICD shocks
| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| Single shock, now well | Interrogation in 24–72 h, check K/Mg, meds |
| **≥2 shocks in 24 h / storm (≥3 VT/VF in 24 h)** | Admit, telemetry, **interrogate**, fix K/Mg, ischemia work-up, amiodarone + β-blocker (propranolol), sedation — see VT chapter |
| **Inappropriate shocks** (AF with RVR, sinus tach, noise) | **Magnet** to suspend, rate-control AF, EP to reprogram/fix the lead |
| Shock + syncope or chest pain | ED, ACS rule-out |
=== Magnet & surgery
- **Above the umbilicus with monopolar cautery** → EMI risk is higher. Pacemaker-dependent patient: magnet (asynchronous) **or reprogram**. ICD: magnet or reprogram to suspend shocks.
- **Below the umbilicus**, bipolar cautery → often no change needed.
- Always: **external pads on**, continuous monitoring, restore and **interrogate after** if reprogrammed or cautery was used near the device.
- Prone position or device in the surgical field → **reprogram**, a magnet may not stay put.
=== MRI, radiation, other
- **MRI:** most current systems are **MR-conditional** → device team programs MRI mode. Legacy (non-conditional) systems can still be scanned under a protocol at experienced centers.
- **Radiation therapy** near the device → device team plan, check after.
- **Cardioversion:** anterior–posterior pads, ≥8 cm from the generator; interrogate after.
- **Central lines:** guidewire in the RV can tangle new leads (<6 weeks) and trigger ICD sensing.
=== Complications
| Early (days–weeks) | Late |
|---|---|
| Pocket hematoma (esp. bridging heparin) | **Device infection** → full system extraction |
| Pneumothorax (subclavian access) | Lead fracture / insulation failure |
| **Lead dislodgement** → loss of capture | Tricuspid regurgitation from the RV lead |
| Perforation → tamponade (chest pain, hypotension) | Pacing-induced cardiomyopathy |
| | Twiddler syndrome (patient rotates generator) |
| | **Leadless:** perforation and groin complications at implant; no pocket or leads to infect |
=== End of life
- Ask early: "If your heart stops at the end of a serious illness, do you want the device to shock you?"
- **Deactivating shocks** is legally and ethically **withdrawing a treatment the patient no longer wants**.
- **Keep pacing on** unless the patient asks (turning off pacing in a dependent patient needs a separate, careful discussion).
- At the bedside without a programmer: **tape a magnet over the ICD** to stop shocks in a dying patient.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Thinking a magnet turns **off** a pacemaker (it makes it pace **all the time**).
- Thinking a magnet changes **pacing** in an ICD (it does not — only tachy therapy).
- Putting a magnet on a **leadless** pacemaker and expecting a response.
- Missing **hyperkalemia** as the cause of loss of capture.
- Forgetting to re-enable ICD therapies after surgery.
- Sending a patient with multiple shocks home without interrogation.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Pacemaker, spikes without QRS, K 7.2 | Hyperkalemia → loss of capture → calcium |
| ICD fires repeatedly during AF with RVR, patient awake and talking | Inappropriate shocks → magnet + rate control |
| Hospice patient with ICD, wants no shocks | Deactivate tachy therapies (magnet if no programmer) |
| Regular paced tachycardia at upper rate after a PVC | Pacemaker-mediated tachycardia → magnet |
| New pacemaker, day 2, sudden hypotension and chest pain | Lead perforation → tamponade → echo |
| Elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy, ICD | Suspend tachy therapy (magnet or reprogram), pads on |
| EF 30%, LBBB 160 ms, NYHA III on GDMT | CRT-D |
