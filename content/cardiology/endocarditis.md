:::bottom
- **Suspect IE** with: bacteremia from a typical organism (**S. aureus**, strep, **E. faecalis**), fever + new murmur, unexplained emboli/stroke, IDU, prosthetic valve or device.
- **3 sets of blood cultures** from separate sites **before antibiotics** (minutes apart if septic).
- **TTE first → TEE** if TTE negative but suspicion remains, **prosthetic valve**, **CIED**, **S. aureus bacteremia**, suspected abscess, or before oral step-down. **PET/CT** or cardiac CT for prosthetic valves.
- **Empiric (native valve):** **vancomycin + ceftriaxone** (or cefepime if healthcare risk). Narrow fast with ID.
- **Call the Endocarditis Team early** (cardiology, CT surgery, ID). Surgery for **HF**, **uncontrolled infection** (abscess, persistent bacteremia), **embolic risk** (vegetation ≥10 mm with emboli).
- **Stable left-sided IE** after ≥10 days IV (and TEE without abscess) → consider **oral step-down** (POET).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Damaged endothelium (jet lesions, prosthetic material, leads) → **platelet–fibrin clot** → bacteria in the blood stick to it → **vegetation**.
- A vegetation has **no blood supply** and a huge bacterial load hidden in fibrin → we need **bactericidal** drugs for **weeks**.
- Three ways it hurts: **destroys the valve** (acute regurgitation → HF), **spreads locally** (annular **abscess** → **heart block**, fistula), **throws emboli** (brain, spleen, kidney, lungs in right-sided IE) and triggers **immune complexes** (GN, Osler nodes, Roth spots).

:::pearl
**New PR prolongation or AV block in aortic-valve IE** = the infection is eating into the tissue near the AV node and His bundle (see the Conduction chapter) → **TEE + surgical consult the same day.**
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{fig:duke-iscvid}}

| Organism | Clue |
|---|---|
| **S. aureus** | Most common. IDU, healthcare, CIED. Acute, destructive. **Always get echo** |
| **Viridans strep** | Subacute, native valves, poor dentition |
| **S. gallolyticus (bovis)** | → **colonoscopy** (colon cancer) |
| **E. faecalis** | Older men, GU procedures, TAVR |
| **CoNS** (S. epidermidis) | Prosthetic valves early, devices |
| **HACEK, Coxiella, Bartonella, Tropheryma** | **Culture-negative** IE → serology, PCR |
| **Candida** | IDU, TPN, central lines, prosthetic valves → surgery |

**Right-sided IE (tricuspid)** in IDU → **septic pulmonary emboli** (multiple cavitating nodules), often few classic signs.

## 🧪 Workup

- **Blood cultures ×3** before antibiotics, then **repeat q24–48 h until negative** (document day of first negative culture).
- **TTE → TEE** (see bottom line). Repeat TEE if new findings (new block, persistent bacteremia).
- **ECG** (PR interval!) daily-ish early in aortic valve IE.
- CBC, BMP, **UA** (GN, hematuria), ESR/CRP, HIV/HCV (IDU).
- **Brain imaging** (CT/MRI) if neuro symptoms — and before surgery, many centers image everyone.
- **CT** for splenic/renal emboli, mycotic aneurysms; **dental exam**.
- **Culture-negative:** stop and think — prior antibiotics? Then *Coxiella*, *Bartonella* serology, 16S PCR on valve tissue.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Empiric
| Situation | Regimen (typical adult) |
|---|---|
| **Native valve**, community | **Vancomycin** (AUC-guided) **+ ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily** |
| Native valve, healthcare-associated / gram-negative risk | Vancomycin + **cefepime** |
| **Prosthetic valve <1 year** | Vancomycin + **gentamicin** + **cefepime**; **add rifampin** only once cultures are negative/known staph |
| **Prosthetic valve ≥1 year** | Like native valve |
=== Targeted (with ID)
| Organism | Typical regimen |
|---|---|
| **MSSA, native** | **Cefazolin 2 g q8h** or nafcillin/oxacillin 2 g q4h × 6 wk |
| **MRSA, native** | **Vancomycin** or **daptomycin 8–10 mg/kg/day** × 6 wk |
| **Staph, prosthetic** | Above **+ rifampin** (≥6 wk) **+ gentamicin** (first 2 wk) |
| **Viridans strep, PCN-sensitive** | **Penicillin G** or **ceftriaxone 2 g daily** × 4 wk (prosthetic: 6 wk) |
| **E. faecalis** | **Ampicillin 2 g q4h + ceftriaxone 2 g q12h** × 6 wk (less nephrotoxic than gentamicin) |
| **HACEK** | Ceftriaxone × 4 wk (native) |

Count duration from the **first negative blood culture** (or from surgery if the valve culture is positive).
=== Surgery — when?
| Indication | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Heart failure** (most common) | Severe acute AR/MR, fistula, valve obstruction → **emergency/urgent** |
| **Uncontrolled infection** | **Abscess**, pseudoaneurysm, fistula, **new heart block**, enlarging vegetation, **bacteremia persisting despite therapy and source control**, fungi, resistant organisms |
| **Prevent emboli** | Vegetation **≥10 mm with ≥1 embolic event**; very large vegetations (>30 mm) |
| **Device (CIED) infection** | **Complete removal of generator and all leads** |

Ischemic stroke **without** large hemorrhage or coma is **not** a reason to delay surgery. Intracranial hemorrhage → usually delay ~4 weeks.
=== Oral step-down & OPAT
- **POET:** stable left-sided IE (strep, E. faecalis, S. aureus, CoNS), **≥10 days IV** (≥7 days after surgery), afebrile, CRP falling, **TEE without abscess** → two oral drugs from different classes for the rest of the course. Outcomes at 5 years were **as good or better** than full IV.
- OPAT is fine for stable patients with home support and weekly labs.
- **Patients who inject drugs:** offer **MOUD** (buprenorphine/methadone) in hospital. Do not refuse surgery or oral therapy on that basis alone — work with addiction medicine.
=== Prevention
- **Who:** prosthetic valve (incl. TAVR) or prosthetic repair material, **prior IE**, certain congenital heart disease, cardiac transplant with valvulopathy. ESC also: transcatheter mitral/tricuspid repair, VAD.
- **When:** dental procedures that manipulate the gingiva or tooth apex, or perforate the mucosa.
- **What:** **amoxicillin 2 g PO** 30–60 min before. PCN allergy → **cephalexin 2 g** (no anaphylaxis history), **azithromycin 500 mg**, or **doxycycline 100 mg**. **Clindamycin is no longer recommended.**
- Not needed for routine GI/GU procedures or before dental work for CIED alone.
:::

:::dose
Typical adult doses. Adjust for kidney function and drug levels with pharmacy/ID. Avoid aminoglycosides in native-valve staph IE (nephrotoxicity, no benefit).
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Starting antibiotics before cultures → culture-negative IE for weeks.
- Treating **S. aureus bacteremia** as a "line infection" without an echo.
- One negative TTE in a prosthetic-valve patient = **not** ruled out.
- Missing a **new heart block** (abscess) — check the PR daily.
- Leaving the **pacemaker leads** in with a device infection.
- Starting anticoagulation for a "stroke" in IE before imaging for hemorrhage.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| IDU, fever, multiple cavitating lung nodules | Tricuspid IE (S. aureus) → septic emboli |
| S. gallolyticus endocarditis | Colonoscopy |
| Aortic valve IE, new 1st-degree AV block | Aortic root abscess → TEE, surgery |
| E. faecalis IE, CKD | Ampicillin + ceftriaxone |
| Culture-negative IE, farm animal exposure | Coxiella burnetii (Q fever) |
| Culture-negative IE, homeless, body lice | Bartonella quintana |
| Pacemaker pocket infection with bacteremia | Remove the whole system |
| Prosthetic valve, dental cleaning | Amoxicillin 2 g PO before |
