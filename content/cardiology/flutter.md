:::bottom
- **Regular narrow tachycardia at ~150?** Think **2:1 flutter** until proven otherwise.
- **Unstable** → synchronized cardioversion (AHA 2025: start **200 J**).
- **Rate control is harder** than in AF. Cardioversion or ablation is often the better plan.
- **Same stroke risk rules as AF** — use CHA₂DS₂-VASc and the same cardioversion clot rules.
- **Typical flutter → refer for CTI ablation** (>90% success).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **One big circuit** (macro-reentry) in the right atrium. In typical flutter the wave runs **counter-clockwise** around the tricuspid valve and through the **cavotricuspid isthmus (CTI)**.
- The atria beat at **~300/min**. The AV node usually passes every 2nd wave → **ventricular rate 150**. With 4:1 block → 75.
- **Why ablation works:** burn a line across the narrow isthmus → the circuit cannot finish its loop.
- Atypical flutter (left atrial, often after AF ablation or surgery) needs mapping by EP.

:::analogy
A race car on an oval track. AF is a demolition derby. Flutter is one car going around the same track again and again. Put a wall across the track (CTI ablation) and the race stops.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{ecg:flutter|Negative sawtooth "F waves" in II, III, aVF. Atrial rate ~300. One F wave often hides in the QRS or T wave.}}

| Clue | What to do |
|---|---|
| Rate exactly 140–160, regular | Look for a second P hidden in the T wave |
| Unclear rhythm | **Adenosine** or vagal maneuver → AV block unmasks the sawtooth (diagnostic, not curative) |
| Lead V1 | Often positive discrete flutter waves |
| Variable block | Irregular rhythm — can look like AF |

## 🧪 Workup

Same as AF: ECG, BMP + Mg, TSH, CBC, TTE. Look for triggers: COPD/lung disease, post-op (cardiac surgery), thyroid, alcohol, PE.

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Unstable?
Y: Synchronized cardioversion 200 J (AHA 2025), increase if no response.
Q: Pre-excitation?
Y: No AV nodal blockers → procainamide / DCCV.
Q: Stable — rate or rhythm?
Y: Rate: metoprolol or diltiazem (same EF rules as AF). Often needs high doses.
N: Rhythm: DCCV (low energy works well), ibutilide, or overdrive pacing (post-op wires, pacemaker).
Q: Anticoagulation decided? Ablation referral?
:::

:::tabs
=== Rate control
- Metoprolol 2.5–5 mg IV q5 min or diltiazem 0.25 mg/kg IV (EF >40%).
- Hard: the AV node can jump from 4:1 to 2:1 → sudden rate change.
- **Class IC drugs (flecainide) without an AV nodal blocker → 1:1 conduction** (rate ~250–300). Dangerous.
=== Rhythm control
- **DCCV** is very effective. Clot rules = AF: ≥48 h or unknown → 3 weeks OAC or TEE.
- **Ibutilide** 1 mg IV over 10 min (repeat once). Watch QTc for 4 h — torsades risk. Give Mg first.
- **CTI ablation** is first-line for recurrent typical flutter.
=== Anticoagulation
- Same as AF (CHA₂DS₂-VASc). DOAC preferred.
- After cardioversion: OAC ≥4 weeks.
- After successful ablation: many patients later get AF too — keep watching.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Calling it "sinus tach 150" and giving fluids for hours.
- Forgetting anticoagulation because "it's just flutter."
- Flecainide alone → 1:1 flutter.
- Ibutilide without QT/Mg check.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Regular narrow tachycardia, HR 150, "sawtooth" II/III/aVF | Atrial flutter 2:1 |
| Best long-term treatment, typical flutter | CTI catheter ablation |
| Flutter on flecainide, HR now 280 | 1:1 conduction — add AV nodal blocker / DCCV |
| Adenosine given, sawtooth appears | Diagnostic for flutter |
