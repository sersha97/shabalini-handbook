:::bottom
- **HFpEF = HF symptoms + EF ≥50% + proof of high filling pressure** (BNP, E/e′, LA size, or invasive).
- Use **H₂FPEF** (or HFA-PEFF) when unsure. Obesity lowers BNP.
- **Rule out mimics:** **ATTR amyloid**, HCM, valve disease, constriction, high-output states.
- **2026 ACC pathway — backbone = SGLT2i + finerenone** (finerenone is the MRA of choice; spironolactone if not feasible).
- **BMI ≥30 → add a GLP-1 RA or tirzepatide** (STEP-HFpEF, SUMMIT).
- Diuretics for congestion. **β-blockers only for a reason** (AF rate, angina, post-MI).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Stiff LV + stiff vessels + **systemic inflammation from visceral fat** → high LV filling pressure at rest or with exercise.
- The 2026 view: HFpEF is a **multisystem cardio-kidney-metabolic syndrome**, not "just diastolic dysfunction."
- Exercise → pressure spikes → lung congestion → dyspnea. AF, HTN, CKD, obesity and anemia all add load.

:::analogy
HFrEF is a weak pump. HFpEF is a strong pump stuck in a too-small, stiff balloon — every extra drop of volume raises pressure a lot.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| H₂FPEF item | Points |
|---|---|
| **H**eavy: BMI >30 | 2 |
| **H**ypertensive: ≥2 BP drugs | 1 |
| **F**ibrillation (AF) | 3 |
| **P**ulmonary HTN: PASP >35 mmHg | 1 |
| **E**lder: age >60 | 1 |
| **F**illing: E/e′ >9 | 1 |

0–1 low probability · 2–5 intermediate (consider BNP, exercise echo / invasive test) · **6–9 high probability**.

:::warn Mimics to hunt
- **ATTR cardiac amyloid:** age >65, bilateral carpal tunnel, spinal stenosis, biceps rupture, low-voltage ECG with thick walls, troponin always a little high → **PYP/DPD scan + serum free light chains & SPEP/UPEP/immunofixation** (rule out AL first). Treatment: tafamidis / acoramidis.
- HCM · moderate–severe AS/MR · constrictive pericarditis · high-output (anemia, AV fistula, thyroid, liver) · pulmonary arterial hypertension.
:::

## 💊 Management (2026 ACC pathway)

| Therapy | Who | Dose / note |
|---|---|---|
| **SGLT2i** (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) | **All** confirmed HFpEF | 10 mg daily |
| **Finerenone** | Most patients (K ≤5.0, eGFR ≥25) | Start 10–20 mg daily by eGFR; titrate; check K in 4 weeks |
| Spironolactone | If finerenone not possible (cost, access) | 12.5–25 mg daily |
| **GLP-1 RA / tirzepatide** | **BMI ≥30** | Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly or tirzepatide — weight, symptoms, HF events |
| ARNI / ARB | Consider, esp. EF in lower range (≤55–60), women | Sacubitril/valsartan 24/26 → 97/103 BID |
| Loop diuretic | Congestion | Lowest dose that keeps euvolemia |
| β-blocker | **Only** with AF, angina, post-MI | Not routine (blunts heart-rate reserve) |

- Treat **BP** (<130/80), **AF** (consider rhythm control), sleep apnea, CKD, diabetes.
- Exercise training and weight loss improve symptoms.

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Labeling every dyspneic obese patient "HFpEF" without objective data.
- Missing amyloid (and starting high-dose β-blockers/digoxin, poorly tolerated).
- Over-diuresis → AKI, hypotension (small, stiff LV is preload-dependent).
- Rate too slow — HFpEF patients need heart-rate reserve.
