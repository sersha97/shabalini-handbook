:::bottom
- **BP >180/120 + acute target-organ damage = hypertensive emergency** → ICU/step-down, **arterial line**, **titratable IV drug**.
- **BP >180/120 without new organ damage = "severe hypertension"** (2025 term — "urgency" is retired). **Do not** treat the number with IV pushes. Restart or adjust oral meds, fix pain, anxiety, urinary retention, withdrawal.
- **No compelling condition:** SBP ↓ **≤25% in the first hour** → **<160/100** over the next 2–6 h → **130–140** systolic over 24–48 h.
- **Compelling conditions:** **aortic dissection SBP <120 + HR <60** in the first hour · **severe preeclampsia/eclampsia and pheochromocytoma SBP <140** in the first hour · **stroke and ICH** have their own targets.
- **Flash pulmonary edema** → high-dose **nitroglycerin** + NIPPV (± loop diuretic).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

{{fig:autoreg}}

- A sudden rise in **vascular resistance** (often after stopping meds, drugs, pain, renal disease) → shear stress → **endothelial injury**, fibrinoid necrosis → more vasoconstriction → a vicious circle.
- Injured small vessels leak and clot → **encephalopathy/PRES**, **retinal hemorrhages**, **AKI**, **thrombotic microangiopathy** (schistocytes).
- Many patients are **volume-depleted** from pressure natriuresis → careful with diuretics unless edema.

## 🔎 Diagnosis — find the organ damage

| Organ | What to look for |
|---|---|
| 🧠 Brain | Headache + confusion, seizures, vision change (**PRES** on MRI), stroke, ICH, SAH |
| 👁️ Eyes | **Flame hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, papilledema** (= "malignant hypertension") |
| ❤️ Heart & aorta | ACS, acute pulmonary edema, **aortic dissection** |
| 🫘 Kidney | AKI, new proteinuria/hematuria |
| 🩸 Blood | **MAHA**: schistocytes, ↓ platelets, ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin |
| 🤰 Pregnancy | **Preeclampsia/eclampsia** (≥20 weeks or up to 6 weeks postpartum), HELLP |

:::warn
A mildly high troponin or creatinine **alone** is not proof of acute organ damage — compare with baseline. Treat the patient, not the number.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

- **ECG**, troponin, **BMP/creatinine**, **UA**, CBC + **smear**, LDH/haptoglobin if low platelets.
- **CXR** if dyspnea. **CT head** (± MRI) for neuro changes. **CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis** if tearing pain or pulse deficit.
- **Fundoscopy** — the cheapest organ-damage test you have.
- **Pregnancy test** in all women of child-bearing age. **Urine tox** (cocaine, amphetamines). Med list: missed clonidine/β-blocker (rebound), MAOI, decongestants, NSAIDs, steroids.

## 💊 Management

{{fig:htn-steps}}

:::tabs
=== IV drugs
| Drug | Typical adult dose | Best for / avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Nicardipine** | 5 mg/h, ↑2.5 mg/h q5–15 min, max 15 mg/h | Most situations, stroke, renal · caution severe AS |
| **Clevidipine** | 1–2 mg/h, double q90 s early, then smaller steps; max 32 mg/h | Fast, short-acting · lipid emulsion — avoid soy/egg allergy, lipid disorders |
| **Labetalol** | 10–20 mg IV, then 20–80 mg q10 min (max 300 mg) or 0.5–2 mg/min | Dissection, stroke, pregnancy · avoid bradycardia, AV block, acute HF, asthma |
| **Esmolol** | 0.5–1 mg/kg load, then 50–300 mcg/kg/min | Dissection, post-op, tachycardia |
| **Nitroglycerin** | 5–20 mcg/min, ↑ q3–5 min; high doses (100–400 mcg/min) for pulmonary edema | ACS, pulmonary edema · avoid PDE-5 inhibitor use, RV infarct |
| **Nitroprusside** | 0.3–0.5 mcg/kg/min, max 10 | Powerful; cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity with high dose, long use, CKD |
| **Phentolamine** | 1–5 mg IV bolus, repeat | Pheochromocytoma, cocaine, MAOI crisis |
| **Hydralazine** | 10–20 mg IV | Mainly pregnancy · unpredictable, reflex tachycardia |
| **Enalaprilat** | 0.625–1.25 mg IV q6h | Slow, variable · avoid pregnancy, AKI |
=== Compelling conditions
| Condition | Target | Preferred |
|---|---|---|
| **Aortic dissection** | SBP **<120**, HR **<60** in first hour (as fast as tolerated) | **β-blocker first** (esmolol/labetalol) → then nicardipine or nitroprusside |
| **Acute pulmonary edema** | Relieve congestion; SBP ↓ ~25% | **Nitroglycerin** (high dose), NIPPV, loop diuretic if overloaded |
| **ACS** | Treat ischemia, SBP <140 | Nitroglycerin, β-blocker (if no HF/shock) |
| **Severe preeclampsia/eclampsia** | SBP **<140** in first hour (≥160/110 = treat within 30–60 min) | **Labetalol, hydralazine, oral nifedipine IR** + **magnesium** · no ACEi/ARB, nitroprusside |
| **Pheo / sympathomimetic** | SBP <140 | **Phentolamine** (or nicardipine) + benzodiazepines · **no β-blocker before α-block** |
| **Acute ischemic stroke — lysis/thrombectomy** | **<185/110** before, **≤180/105** for 24 h after · **do not push SBP <140** (no benefit after IVT, harm after EVT) | Labetalol, nicardipine, clevidipine |
| **Acute ischemic stroke — no reperfusion** | Treat only if **>220/120** (lower ~15% in 24 h) | Same |
| **ICH** | SBP 150–220 → aim **~140** (keep 130–150); avoid <130 | Nicardipine, clevidipine, labetalol |
=== Severe HTN on the wards
- **Recheck** with the right cuff, patient calm, 5 min rest.
- Look for the cause: **pain, anxiety, full bladder, hypoxia, withdrawal (alcohol, clonidine, opioids), held home meds**.
- **No IV antihypertensive "for the number"** in patients admitted for something else without organ damage — **2025 guideline: harm** (more hypotension, AKI, stroke risk, no benefit).
- Restart/up-titrate **oral long-acting** drugs. Aim for a gradual fall over **days**, then outpatient follow-up in 1 week.
- **Never sublingual nifedipine IR** outside pregnancy protocols → stroke, MI.
:::

:::dose
Typical adult doses. Titrate to the target, not to "normal." Watch for overshoot — especially elderly, volume-depleted, and chronic hypertensives.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Treating a number in an asymptomatic inpatient with IV hydralazine → hypotension, falls, AKI.
- Lowering BP to normal in an hour → watershed stroke, MI, AKI.
- Vasodilator **without β-blocker** in dissection → reflex tachycardia, propagates the tear.
- β-blocker alone in **cocaine** or **pheo** → unopposed α → BP spike.
- Missing **pregnancy/postpartum** preeclampsia (up to 6 weeks after delivery).
- Forgetting the **clonidine** the patient stopped yesterday (rebound).

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| BP 210/130, confusion, seizure, MRI parieto-occipital edema | PRES → nicardipine, lower 25% in 1 h |
| BP 200/120, no symptoms, admitted for cellulitis | Severe HTN → adjust oral meds, no IV push |
| Tearing back pain, BP 190/110, HR 110 | Esmolol first → then nicardipine; SBP <120 in 1 h |
| Postpartum day 5, BP 168/112, headache | Preeclampsia → labetalol/nifedipine + magnesium |
| BP 220/130 after cocaine | Benzodiazepine + phentolamine (or nicardipine) |
| Ischemic stroke, tPA candidate, BP 200/110 | Lower to <185/110 before tPA |
| Malignant HTN, schistocytes, low platelets, AKI | Hypertensive TMA → controlled BP lowering |
