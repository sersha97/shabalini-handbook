:::bottom
- **Troponin up ≠ NSTEMI.** Ask: is there **acute ischemia from a plaque event** (type 1), a **supply–demand problem** (type 2), or **non-ischemic injury**?
- Use **hs-troponin 0/1-h or 0/2-h** pathways. Need a **rise and/or fall** for acute injury.
- **Type 1 NSTEMI:** aspirin + anticoagulant (heparin/enoxaparin) + high-intensity statin; P2Y12 per cath timing.
- **Cath timing:** immediate (<2 h) if unstable · early (<24 h) if high risk (GRACE >140) · before discharge for the rest.
- **Type 2 MI:** treat the cause (anemia, sepsis, AF-RVR, hypotension). No reflex heparin/cath.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 1 MI** | Plaque rupture/erosion → **partial** thrombus | Classic NSTEMI |
| **Type 2 MI** | O₂ supply/demand mismatch **with** ischemia | AF-RVR, GI bleed, hypotension |
| **Myocardial injury** | Troponin up, **no** ischemia | Sepsis, PE, myocarditis, CKD, HF, stroke |
| Type 3 / 4 / 5 | Sudden death / PCI-related / CABG-related | |

## 🔎 Diagnosis

**MI = rise/fall of troponin (≥1 value >99th percentile) + ischemia** (symptoms, new ECG changes, new Q waves, imaging wall-motion loss, or thrombus at angiography).

- **hs-cTn algorithms** (assay-specific): very low at 0 h (with >3 h of symptoms) → rule out · small delta at 1–2 h → rule out · high value or big delta → rule in · middle → observe, repeat at 3 h.
- **ECG:** ST depression, T inversion, transient STE. Repeat if pain returns.
- Unstable angina: ischemic symptoms, **normal troponin**.

| Score | Use |
|---|---|
| **HEART** (0–10) | ED chest pain: 0–3 low risk → discharge pathway |
| **GRACE** | NSTE-ACS mortality; **>140 → early invasive** |
| TIMI (0–7) | Older; simple |

## 💊 Management (type 1 NSTE-ACS)

:::tabs
=== Meds
- **Aspirin** 162–325 mg → 81 mg.
- **Anticoagulant:** UFH (60 U/kg bolus, 12 U/kg/h) or **enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h** (daily if CrCl <30). Fondaparinux needs extra UFH at PCI.
- **P2Y12:** ticagrelor or prasugrel preferred over clopidogrel. **Prasugrel only after anatomy known.** If cath will be **>24 h away**, early clopidogrel or ticagrelor is reasonable (2025).
- **High-intensity statin** day 1. β-blocker (no HF/shock/bradycardia). Nitro for pain.
=== Cath timing
| Timing | Who |
|---|---|
| **Immediate <2 h** | Refractory angina, shock/unstable, VT/VF, acute HF, mechanical complication |
| **Early <24 h** | GRACE >140, dynamic ST changes, troponin rising quickly |
| **Inpatient** | Intermediate risk |
| **Selective / non-invasive first** | Low risk, frail, major bleed risk — shared decision |
=== Type 2 MI
- Fix the trigger: transfuse (see anemia page), control AF rate, treat sepsis/hypoxia, stop hypotension.
- Antiplatelet/heparin **only** if type 1 is still likely.
- Afterwards: outpatient ischemia testing if CAD likely; statin if CAD found.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Heparin drip for every troponin bump (sepsis, CKD, PE).
- Single troponin without a delta.
- Missing **posterior MI** (ST depression V1–V3 can be an OMI → cath now).
- Stress testing someone with Wellens T waves.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| GI bleed, Hb 6, troponin up, no chest pain | Type 2 MI → transfuse, treat bleed |
| NSTEMI, refractory chest pain on drip | Immediate cath (<2 h) |
| NSTEMI, GRACE 160, stable | Early invasive within 24 h |
| Troponin elevated, flat, ESRD, no symptoms | Chronic myocardial injury |
| NSTEMI, cath tomorrow, prior ICH | Avoid prasugrel; consider clopidogrel/ticagrelor and bleed risk |
