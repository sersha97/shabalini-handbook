:::bottom
- **QTc >500 ms or rise >60 ms** = high risk → stop QT drugs, replete **K >4, Mg >2**, telemetry.
- **Torsades with a pulse** → **magnesium 2 g IV** (even if Mg is normal), repeat once.
- **Pulseless / unstable torsades** → **defibrillate** (unsynchronized — it cannot sync).
- Recurrent torsades → **overdrive pacing** (rate ~100) or **isoproterenol** (raise HR to shorten QT).
- Check the **med list**: antiarrhythmics, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, ondansetron, IV haloperidol, methadone, antipsychotics.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- QT = depolarization + repolarization. Long QT = **slow repolarization** (blocked K⁺ channel, mostly hERG / IKr).
- Slow repolarization → **early afterdepolarizations** → a beat lands on the T wave → **torsades de pointes**.
- **Pause-dependent:** short–long–short R–R sequence starts it. Bradycardia makes it worse.
- Risk stacks: **female**, age, HF, low K/Mg/Ca, bradycardia, **two QT drugs**, congenital LQTS.

## 🔎 Measure it right

{{ecg:longqt|T wave ends more than halfway to the next QRS → QT is probably long.}}

| Method | Formula | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bazett | QT ÷ √RR | Over-corrects at fast HR |
| Fridericia | QT ÷ ∛RR | Better at HR <60 or >90 |
| Wide QRS | Use JT (QT − QRS) or subtract (QRS − 100) | BBB/paced rhythms inflate QT |

- Measure in **lead II or V5**, use the **tangent method** (steepest T downslope → baseline). Do not trust the machine with U waves or AF.
- Normal: men <450, women <460 ms.

{{ecg:torsades|Torsades: polymorphic VT with QRS twisting around the baseline.}}

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Long QT, no arrhythmia
- Stop or swap QT drugs (check CredibleMeds).
- K to **4–5**, Mg **>2**.
- Telemetry if QTc >500.
- Repeat ECG after changes.
- Avoid bradycardia.
=== Torsades
- **Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV** over 1–2 min (if pulse), then infusion 1–4 g/h may be used. Watch for loss of reflexes in CKD.
- **Defibrillate** if pulseless or unstable.
- **Overdrive pacing** 90–110/min (transvenous) — best for recurrent episodes.
- **Isoproterenol** 2–10 mcg/min if pacing not available (not in congenital LQTS or ischemia).
- Lidocaine may help. **Avoid amiodarone/procainamide** (prolong QT).
=== Safer swaps
| Instead of | Consider |
|---|---|
| IV haloperidol for agitation | Low-dose olanzapine/quetiapine with ECG, or non-drug measures |
| Ondansetron (repeated IV doses) | Lowest dose, PO route, treat the cause; most antiemetics prolong QT — recheck ECG |
| Azithromycin / levofloxacin | Doxycycline, β-lactam (depending on infection) |
| Methadone high dose | Check ECG; consider buprenorphine with addiction team |
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Machine QTc in AF or with wide QRS.
- Adding "just one more" QT drug at night (ondansetron + haloperidol + levofloxacin).
- Using **synchronized** cardioversion for torsades (cannot find an R wave).
- Forgetting **hypocalcemia** and **hypothyroidism**.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Torsades, BP 100/60, Mg normal | Magnesium sulfate IV |
| Recurrent torsades after Mg | Overdrive pacing / isoproterenol |
| Congenital LQTS, syncope with loud noise (alarm clock) | LQT2 → β-blocker, ICD if recurrent |
| Swimming-triggered syncope | LQT1 → β-blocker (nadolol/propranolol) |
| QTc 520 on methadone + azithromycin | Stop azithro, check K/Mg, reassess methadone |
