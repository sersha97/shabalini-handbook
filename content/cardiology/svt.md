:::bottom
- **Unstable** → synchronized cardioversion **100 J**.
- **Stable, regular, narrow** → **modified Valsalva** first → **adenosine 6 mg → 12 mg → 12 mg** rapid push with flush.
- Adenosine fails or SVT returns → **diltiazem/verapamil** or **β-blocker** (if EF OK).
- **Irregular wide tachycardia = maybe pre-excited AF** → never adenosine or AV nodal blockers.
- Recurrent SVT → refer for **ablation** (cure rate ~95%).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

| Type | Circuit | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **AVNRT** | Re-entry inside the AV node (slow + fast pathway) | ~60% |
| **AVRT** | Loop through AV node + accessory pathway (WPW) | ~30% |
| **Atrial tachycardia** | Focus in the atrium (not AV node-dependent) | ~10% |

- AVNRT and AVRT **need the AV node** → anything that blocks the node (vagal, adenosine) can **break** them.
- Atrial tachycardia continues with AV block → adenosine only slows the rate and shows the P waves.

:::analogy
AVNRT is a dog chasing its tail inside the AV node. Adenosine freezes the dog for a few seconds — the circle stops.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{ecg:svt|Regular, narrow, ~150–250/min. P waves hidden or just after QRS ("pseudo-r' in V1" in AVNRT).}}

- **WPW in sinus rhythm:** short PR (<120 ms) + **delta wave** + wide QRS.
- Orthodromic AVRT (most common in WPW) → **narrow** QRS during SVT. Antidromic → wide.
- Long RP tachycardia → think atrial tachycardia, PJRT, atypical AVNRT.

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Unstable (hypotension, shock, ischemia, AMS)?
Y: Synchronized cardioversion 100 J. Sedate if time allows.
Q: Regular and narrow?
Y: Modified Valsalva → adenosine 6 mg → 12 mg → 12 mg.
N: Wide or irregular → see VT / pre-excited AF pages.
Q: Still in SVT or recurs?
Y: Diltiazem 0.25 mg/kg IV or verapamil 5–10 mg IV (EF normal) · metoprolol 2.5–5 mg IV.
Q: Long-term plan
Y: Recurrent or WPW → EP for ablation. Occasional → "pill in pocket" or daily BB/CCB.
:::

:::tabs
=== Modified Valsalva (REVERT)
1. Patient semi-recumbent (45°).
2. Blow into a 10 mL syringe for **15 seconds** (40 mmHg strain).
3. **Right away** lay flat and raise the legs to 45° for 15 s.
4. Return to semi-recumbent. Check rhythm.

~43% conversion vs ~17% with standard Valsalva.
=== Adenosine
- **6 mg rapid IV push** + immediate 10–20 mL flush (antecubital line). Then 12 mg, then 12 mg.
- **3 mg** if central line, heart transplant, dipyridamole or carbamazepine.
- Caffeine/theophylline block it → may need more.
- Warn the patient: chest pressure, flushing, "sense of doom", a few seconds of asystole.
- **Record a rhythm strip** during the push — it is diagnostic.
- ⛔ Irregular or wide-complex tachycardia, severe bronchospasm.
=== WPW special rules
- Orthodromic AVRT (narrow) → adenosine/CCB OK.
- **Pre-excited AF** (irregular, wide, very fast) → **procainamide** or DCCV. **No** adenosine, BB, CCB, digoxin, IV amiodarone.
- Any symptomatic WPW → refer for ablation.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Slow push of adenosine through a hand IV → no effect.
- Sinus tachycardia called "SVT" — look for a cause (pain, fever, bleed, PE) and gradual onset.
- Verapamil in wide-complex tachycardia → can cause collapse (VT).

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Young woman, sudden palpitations, HR 190 regular narrow, stable | Vagal maneuver → adenosine |
| Short PR + delta wave, palpitations | WPW → EP study/ablation |
| Irregular wide tachycardia HR 260 in WPW | Procainamide or DCCV |
| Adenosine → rhythm slows, sawtooth appears | Atrial flutter |
| SVT on theophylline, adenosine fails | Methylxanthine blocks adenosine |
