:::bottom
- **History + exam + ECG** explain most syncope. Everyone gets an **ECG** and **orthostatic vitals**.
- **Red flags for cardiac syncope:** exertion, supine, no prodrome, palpitations before, structural heart disease, family history of sudden death, abnormal ECG.
- **High risk → admit with telemetry**, TTE. Low-risk reflex syncope → home.
- **Do not order** routine head CT, EEG or carotid ultrasound for syncope.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

Syncope = **transient global brain hypoperfusion** → fast loss of consciousness + tone → **fast, full, spontaneous recovery**.

| Type | Mechanism | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflex** (vasovagal, situational, carotid sinus) | Vagal surge → brady + vasodilation | Standing, heat, pain, blood, cough/micturition; prodrome (warm, nausea, sweat); young |
| **Orthostatic** | Not enough vasoconstriction on standing | Drugs (α-blockers, diuretics), volume loss, autonomic failure (PD, DM) |
| **Cardiac** | Arrhythmia or obstruction | Exertional, supine, sudden, older, heart disease |

:::warn Not syncope
Seizure (prolonged confusion, tongue bite on side, head turn), hypoglycemia, intoxication, stroke/TIA (rarely causes LOC), psychogenic, falls without LOC.
:::

## 🔎 The ECG checklist

| Look for | Think |
|---|---|
| Brady <40, pauses >3 s, Mobitz II / CHB | Conduction disease → pacemaker |
| **Bifascicular block** (RBBB + LAFB) | Intermittent CHB |
| Short PR + delta wave | WPW |
| QTc >470–500 | Long QT |
| **Brugada** (coved STE V1–V2) | Ventricular arrhythmia |
| LVH + deep septal Q | **HCM** |
| Epsilon wave, TWI V1–V3 | ARVC |
| Q waves | Prior MI → scar VT |

**Orthostatic hypotension:** SBP ↓ ≥20 or DBP ↓ ≥10 within 3 min of standing (or symptoms). HR ↑ ≥30 without BP drop → POTS.

## 🧪 Workup — targeted

- **Labs:** CBC (bleed), BMP, glucose, pregnancy test. Troponin/BNP only if suspicion.
- **TTE:** murmur, abnormal ECG, known/suspected heart disease.
- **Telemetry** for inpatients; **ambulatory monitor** (Zio 14–30 d, loop recorder) for infrequent events.
- Exercise test if exertional. EP study selected (bifascicular block, prior MI).
- **Canadian Syncope Risk Score** or San Francisco rule (CHESS: CHF, Hct <30, ECG abnormal, SOB, SBP <90) to support disposition.

## 💊 Management

| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Reflex | Reassure, hydrate, salt; counter-pressure maneuvers (leg crossing, hand grip); stop vasodilators |
| Orthostatic | Stop offending drugs, fluids/salt, compression stockings, raise head of bed; fludrocortisone/midodrine/droxidopa if needed |
| Brady / AV block | Pacemaker if no reversible cause |
| VT / structural | ICD, ablation, treat substrate |
| Aortic stenosis / HCM | Valve replacement / HCM-specific therapy |

:::pearl
**Driving:** varies by state. Counsel and document. Reflex syncope with prodrome usually no restriction; unexplained or arrhythmic syncope → restriction until treated.
:::

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Young woman fainted in church, nausea and sweating before | Vasovagal → reassurance |
| 70-year-old, syncope while jogging, systolic murmur | Aortic stenosis → TTE |
| Syncope, RBBB + LAFB on ECG | Intermittent CHB → monitor / EP / pacemaker |
| Elderly man faints after urinating at night | Situational (micturition) syncope |
| Syncope while lying down | Arrhythmia until proven otherwise |
