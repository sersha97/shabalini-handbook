:::bottom
- **Wide + regular + fast = VT** until proven otherwise — especially with prior MI or low EF.
- **No pulse** → ACLS (defibrillate, CPR, epinephrine, amiodarone).
- **Pulse but unstable** → synchronized cardioversion **100 J**.
- **Stable monomorphic VT** → **procainamide** (or amiodarone) + prepare to cardiovert.
- **Polymorphic VT:** long QT → **magnesium** (torsades). Normal QT → think **ischemia** → cath lab.
- Fix **K >4, Mg >2**. Never verapamil/diltiazem for WCT.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Scar re-entry** (old MI, cardiomyopathy) → monomorphic VT. Scar = one fixed circuit → same QRS shape every beat.
- **Ischemia, long QT, electrolytes** → polymorphic VT / torsades.
- The ventricle depolarizes muscle-to-muscle, not through the fast His-Purkinje system → **wide QRS**.
- Fast rate + loss of AV synchrony → low output → collapse → VF.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{ecg:vt|Regular wide-complex tachycardia. Clues for VT: AV dissociation, capture or fusion beats, very wide QRS (>160 ms), extreme axis.}}

| Points for VT | Points for SVT with aberrancy |
|---|---|
| Prior MI, HF, ICD, age >35 | Young, no heart disease, known BBB |
| AV dissociation, fusion/capture beats | Same QRS as baseline BBB |
| QRS >160 ms, northwest axis | Typical RBBB/LBBB pattern |
| Concordance in precordial leads | Terminates with adenosine |

:::pearl
Hemodynamic stability does **not** mean it is SVT. Many patients walk in with stable VT.
:::

## 🧪 Workup

ECG (compare with old), BMP, Mg, troponin, TTE (EF, scar), drug list (QT, digoxin), ICD interrogation if present, ischemia evaluation (cath or stress), cardiac MRI for new cardiomyopathy (sarcoid, myocarditis).

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Stable monomorphic
- **Procainamide** 20–50 mg/min IV until stops, hypotension, QRS widens >50%, or 17 mg/kg. (PROCAMIO: fewer major adverse events than amiodarone.)
- **Amiodarone** 150 mg IV over 10 min, repeat as needed → 1 mg/min ×6 h → 0.5 mg/min ×18 h. Best choice in low EF.
- Lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV — alternative, especially with ischemia.
- Sedate and **synchronized cardioversion 100 J** if drugs fail.
=== Unstable / pulseless
- Pulse + unstable → **sync 100 J**, increase as needed.
- Pulseless VT/VF → **defibrillate** (unsynchronized), CPR, epinephrine 1 mg q3–5 min, amiodarone 300 mg → 150 mg (or lidocaine).
=== Polymorphic
- **Long QT (torsades)** → Mg 2 g IV, stop QT drugs, overdrive pacing / isoproterenol. See QT page.
- **Normal QT** → ischemia until proven otherwise → treat as ACS, urgent cath. Amiodarone, β-blocker.
=== VT storm (≥3 episodes / 24 h)
- Amiodarone + **β-blocker (propranolol)** — block sympathetic drive.
- **Sedation** (deep sedation / intubation reduces adrenergic tone).
- Fix K, Mg, ischemia, HF. Reprogram ICD. Call EP: urgent **ablation**, stellate ganglion block.
:::

:::dose
Typical adult doses. Procainamide: reduce in kidney disease (NAPA). Avoid procainamide with long QT or decompensated HF.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Giving **verapamil/diltiazem** for "SVT with aberrancy" → hemodynamic collapse.
- Forgetting to check **K and Mg**.
- Not asking: "Does this patient have an ICD?" → interrogate.
- Missing ischemia as the cause of polymorphic VT.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Prior MI, stable WCT 170, BP 120/70 | Monomorphic VT → procainamide/amiodarone |
| Same, BP 70/40, awake | Synchronized cardioversion |
| Polymorphic VT, QTc 580 | Magnesium sulfate IV |
| Polymorphic VT, normal QT, chest pain | Ischemia → urgent cath |
| Secondary prevention after VT arrest (no reversible cause) | ICD |
| EF ≤35% ≥40 d post-MI on GDMT, NYHA II–III | Primary-prevention ICD |
