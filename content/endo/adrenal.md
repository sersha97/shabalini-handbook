:::bottom
- **Suspected adrenal crisis** (shock not responding to fluids, vomiting, abdominal pain, low Na/high K, known steroid use) → **hydrocortisone 100 mg IV now**, then **50 mg IV q6h** (or 200 mg/24 h infusion). **Do not wait for labs.**
- Draw **cortisol + ACTH** before the dose if it takes no time; dexamethasone does not cross-react with assays if you must test later.
- **1–2 L saline** (or D5NS if hypoglycemic).
- **Anyone on ≥5 mg prednisone for ≥4 weeks** may have adrenal suppression → stress-dose for major illness/surgery.
- Sick-day rules + emergency card + injection kit at discharge.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

| Type | Problem | Mineralocorticoid? | Clues |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (Addison) | Adrenal gland destroyed (autoimmune, TB, hemorrhage, metastases, drugs) | **Lost** → high K, low Na, salt craving | **Hyperpigmentation**, high ACTH |
| **Secondary/tertiary** | Pituitary/hypothalamus — **most often exogenous steroids** | Preserved (RAAS intact) | No hyperpigmentation, low/normal ACTH |

- Cortisol is needed for vascular tone (it "permits" catecholamines to work) → without it, **shock that ignores pressors**.
- Triggers for crisis: infection, surgery, vomiting, stopping steroids abruptly, starting levothyroxine before steroids.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Test | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Morning cortisol** (8 AM) | <3–5 mcg/dL → AI likely · >15 → unlikely (assay-dependent) |
| **ACTH stimulation** (cosyntropin 250 mcg) | Cortisol at 30/60 min <18 mcg/dL (or assay-specific cut-off ~14–15 for newer assays) → AI |
| ACTH | High = primary · low/normal = secondary |
| Renin/aldosterone | Primary: high renin |
| 21-hydroxylase antibodies | Autoimmune Addison |
| CT adrenals | Hemorrhage (anticoagulated, sepsis, APS), metastases, TB |

:::pearl
Random cortisol in critical illness is hard to read (low albumin, stress). In shock, **treat first**.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Adrenal crisis
- Hydrocortisone **100 mg IV bolus → 50 mg IV q6h** (or 200 mg/day infusion).
- Fluids: 1 L NS in first hour, then by need; glucose for hypoglycemia.
- At 100–200 mg/day hydrocortisone, **no fludrocortisone needed** (enough mineralocorticoid effect).
- Treat trigger. Taper to maintenance in 1–3 days when stable.
=== Maintenance
- **Hydrocortisone 15–25 mg/day** in 2–3 doses (largest in the morning) — or prednisolone 3–5 mg daily.
- Primary AI: **fludrocortisone 50–200 mcg daily** (titrate to BP, K, renin).
=== Stress dosing (peri-op / illness)
| Stress | Dose |
|---|---|
| Minor illness (fever) | **Double** oral dose for 2–3 days ("sick-day rule") |
| Vomiting | IM hydrocortisone 100 mg, then ED |
| Minor surgery | Usual dose (± 25–50 mg HC day of procedure) |
| **Major surgery / ICU** | **Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV at induction → 200 mg/24 h**, taper over 1–3 days |
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Waiting for the stim test while the patient is in shock.
- Starting **levothyroxine before steroids** in combined deficiency (pituitary) → crisis.
- Forgetting stress dosing in long-term prednisone users (COPD, RA, transplant).
- **Etomidate, ketoconazole, mitotane, checkpoint inhibitors** (hypophysitis) as hidden causes.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Hypotension, hyperpigmentation, Na 126, K 5.8 | Primary AI → hydrocortisone now |
| Shock after stopping long-term prednisone | Secondary AI (exogenous) → hydrocortisone |
| Anticoagulated, sepsis, flank pain, shock | Bilateral adrenal hemorrhage |
| Hypopituitarism, start which hormone first? | Glucocorticoid before thyroid hormone |
| Ipilimumab, fatigue, headache, low ACTH | Hypophysitis |
