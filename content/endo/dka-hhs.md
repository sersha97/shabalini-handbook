:::bottom
- **DKA (2024):** glucose ≥200 **or** known diabetes · **ketones** (β-OHB ≥3.0 mmol/L or urine 2+) · **pH <7.3 and/or HCO₃ <18**. Euglycemic DKA counts (SGLT2i!).
- **Fluids first:** 1–1.5 L isotonic crystalloid in hour 1 (balanced crystalloid fine).
- **K <3.5 → hold insulin, give K.** K 3.5–5.0 → add 20–40 mEq/L. K >5 → no K, recheck in 2 h.
- **Insulin 0.1 U/kg/h** (or 0.14 U/kg/h, no bolus). **Glucose <250 → add D5–D10 and halve the insulin**, but **keep it running** until ketosis clears.
- **Resolution:** β-OHB <0.6 + pH ≥7.3 (or HCO₃ ≥18). **Give SC basal insulin 1–2 h before** stopping the drip.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **No insulin + high counter-regulatory hormones** (glucagon, cortisol, catecholamines) → lipolysis → free fatty acids → liver makes **ketones** → anion-gap acidosis.
- High glucose → **osmotic diuresis** → huge water and K losses (whole-body K deficit even when serum K looks high).
- **HHS:** some insulin present (blocks ketosis) but severe hyperglycemia + dehydration → very high osmolality → AMS. Older type 2 patients. Higher mortality.
- **Insulin pushes K into cells** → serum K can crash → arrhythmia. That is why the K rule exists.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| | DKA | HHS |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | ≥200 (or known DM) | **≥600** |
| Ketones (β-OHB) | **≥3.0** | <3.0 |
| pH / HCO₃ | <7.3 and/or <18 | ≥7.3 / ≥15 |
| Effective osmolality | Variable | **>300** (2Na + glucose/18) |
| Mental status | Alert → stupor | Often stupor/coma |

**Find the trigger ("5 I's"):** **I**nsulin missed · **I**nfection · **I**nfarction (MI, stroke) · **I**ntoxication/drugs (SGLT2i, steroids, antipsychotics, cocaine) · **I**nfant (pregnancy). Also pancreatitis, new-onset DM.

Corrected Na = Na + 1.6 × (glucose − 100)/100.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Fluids
- Hour 1: 1–1.5 L isotonic (LR / Plasma-Lyte or NS).
- Then 250–500 mL/h guided by volume status; switch to 0.45% saline if **corrected Na** is normal/high.
- **HHS:** fluids are the main therapy; slower Na fall (≤10 mEq/L/24 h) and osmolality fall (3–8 mOsm/kg/h).
=== Potassium
| Serum K | Action |
|---|---|
| **<3.5** | **Hold insulin.** Give 10–20 mEq/h K until >3.5 |
| 3.5–5.0 | 20–40 mEq K per liter of fluid |
| >5.0 | No K; recheck q2h |
=== Insulin
- **Regular insulin IV 0.1 U/kg/h** (bolus 0.1 optional) or 0.14 U/kg/h without bolus.
- Goal glucose fall 50–75 mg/dL/h.
- Glucose <250 (DKA) / <300 (HHS) → **D5 or D10** + insulin 0.05 U/kg/h.
- **Mild DKA** (alert, pH ≥7.25): SC rapid-acting insulin protocol is acceptable.
- Early **basal insulin** (glargine 0.15–0.3 U/kg) alongside the drip can shorten the drip and reduce rebound.
=== Bicarbonate & phosphate
- Bicarbonate only if **pH <7.0** (and give with K).
- Phosphate only if <1.0 mg/dL or cardiac/respiratory weakness.
- Mg if low.
:::

:::warn Transition
Stop drip only **after** SC basal (± prandial) insulin is on board for **1–2 hours**. Home dose, or 0.5–0.6 U/kg/day split ~50% basal / 50% prandial for new patients.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Starting insulin with K 3.1 → arrhythmia.
- Stopping the drip when glucose normalizes (ketones still high) → rebound DKA.
- Following bicarbonate alone (hyperchloremic NAGMA from saline keeps it low) → use **β-OHB / anion gap**.
- Missing euglycemic DKA on SGLT2i (glucose 180, pH 7.1).
- Restarting SGLT2i before the patient is eating and well.
