:::bottom
- **Delirium = acute, fluctuating inattention** + disorganized thinking or altered level of consciousness. Screen with **CAM** or **4AT**.
- It is a **medical emergency**: look for infection, drugs, retention, constipation, hypoxia, pain, sodium, glucose, withdrawal.
- **Non-drug bundle first** (HELP): glasses, hearing aids, orientation, sleep, mobility, hydration, family.
- **Antipsychotics do not shorten delirium.** Use **low dose** only for distress or danger to self/others.
- **Avoid benzodiazepines** (except alcohol/benzo withdrawal), anticholinergics, and restraints.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Vulnerable brain + stressor.** Dementia, age, frailty, sensory loss lower the threshold; a small trigger (UTI, oxycodone) tips it over.
- Mechanism: neuroinflammation + **acetylcholine deficiency** + dopamine excess + disrupted sleep–wake.
- **Hypoactive delirium** (quiet, sleepy) is most common and most often missed — and has worse outcomes.

:::analogy
The brain is a dam. Age and dementia lower the wall. A storm (infection, drugs, surgery) that a young brain handles easily now floods over.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| CAM (needs 1 + 2 + either 3 or 4) |
|---|
| **1. Acute onset / fluctuating course** |
| **2. Inattention** (months backward, days of week backward, "SAVEAHAART" test) |
| 3. Disorganized thinking |
| 4. Altered level of consciousness |

**4AT** (≥4 = likely delirium): alertness · AMT4 (age, DOB, place, year) · attention (months backward) · acute change.

| Common causes — **"DELIRIUMS"** |
|---|
| **D**rugs (anticholinergics, benzos, opioids, steroids, antihistamines) · **E**lectrolytes (Na, Ca, glucose) · **L**ack of drugs (withdrawal) · **I**nfection · **R**educed sensory input · **I**ntracranial (stroke, bleed, seizure) · **U**rinary retention / constipation · **M**yocardial / lungs (MI, hypoxia, hypercapnia) · **S**leep deprivation, pain |

Workup: med review (**Beers list**), vitals, glucose, BMP, Ca, CBC, UA (only if urinary symptoms or no other cause), bladder scan, CXR, ECG, ABG/VBG if somnolent. **Head CT** if focal signs, fall, anticoagulated, or no cause found. EEG if NCSE suspected.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Non-drug (do all)
- Glasses, hearing aids, clock, calendar, windows/daylight.
- Family at bedside; consistent staff.
- **Sleep protocol:** no vitals 0000–0500 if safe, lights off, noise down.
- Early mobility; remove lines, tubes, catheters, telemetry when possible.
- Hydration, nutrition, bowel regimen, pain control (scheduled acetaminophen).
=== Medications (only if dangerous/distressed)
| Drug | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Haloperidol** | 0.25–0.5 mg PO/IV/IM, repeat q30–60 min, max ~3–5 mg/day | Check QTc; avoid in Parkinson/Lewy body |
| **Quetiapine** | 12.5–25 mg PO | Preferred in **Parkinson / Lewy body** |
| Olanzapine | 2.5–5 mg | Sedating; ODT available |
| Melatonin | 3 mg qHS | Sleep (prevention evidence mixed) |
| Dexmedetomidine | ICU | Agitated delirium in ventilated patients |

Stop antipsychotics at discharge or within days — do not continue "for sundowning."
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- "Sundowning" label without workup.
- **Lorazepam** for agitation (worsens delirium) — except withdrawal.
- Diphenhydramine for sleep.
- Missing **hypoactive** delirium; missing urinary retention.
- Physical restraints (more agitation, injury).

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 82-year-old post-op, inattentive, fluctuating | Delirium → find cause, non-drug measures |
| Delirium + Parkinson disease, agitated | Low-dose quetiapine (avoid haloperidol) |
| Agitated delirium from alcohol withdrawal | Benzodiazepines |
| Best evidence for prevention | Multicomponent non-drug program (HELP) |
| Somnolent, inattentive, quiet elderly patient | Hypoactive delirium |
