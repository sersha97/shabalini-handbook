:::bottom
- **Ask before you tell.** "What do you understand about your illness?"
- Use **REMAP**: **R**eframe · **E**xpect emotion · **M**ap values · **A**lign · **P**lan.
- Recommend a plan **based on their values** — do not hand over a menu of procedures.
- Code status is a **medical recommendation**, not a checkbox. CPR in advanced illness rarely restores the life people hope for.
- At the end of life: **treat dyspnea and pain with opioids**, secretions with glycopyrrolate, agitation with haloperidol/midazolam.
:::

## 🧠 Why this matters

- People overestimate CPR success (TV ~70%). In-hospital arrest survival to discharge is ~20–25% overall and much lower in advanced cancer, frailty, multi-organ failure.
- Clear, early conversations → less ICU at end of life, less family PTSD, more goal-concordant care.

## 🗺️ The talk — REMAP

| Step | Say |
|---|---|
| **Set up** | "Is it OK if we talk about where things are going?" Who should be there? |
| **R**eframe | "I'm worried that time may be short." "Things have changed." |
| **E**xpect emotion | Pause. Name it: "This is hard to hear." **NURSE**: Name, Understand, Respect, Support, Explore |
| **M**ap | "What is most important to you now?" "What are you hoping for? What are you worried about?" "What would be unacceptable?" |
| **A**lign | "So what I hear is that being at home with family matters most…" |
| **P**lan | "Given that, I recommend we focus on comfort and not use CPR or a breathing machine. How does that sound?" |

:::pearl
Use **"I wish… I worry…"** — "I wish the treatment had worked. I worry we are running out of time." Avoid "Do you want us to do everything?"
:::

## 💊 Comfort care orders

| Symptom | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Dyspnea** | Opioid (morphine 2–4 mg IV or 5–10 mg PO q1h prn; hydromorphone if renal failure), fan, positioning, O₂ only if it helps comfort |
| **Pain** | Scheduled opioid + breakthrough (10–20% of 24 h dose) |
| **Secretions ("death rattle")** | Reposition; **glycopyrrolate 0.2–0.4 mg IV/SC q4h**; atropine 1% drops SL; reassure family |
| **Agitation / terminal delirium** | Haloperidol 0.5–2 mg; midazolam 1–2 mg if refractory |
| Nausea | Haloperidol, ondansetron, metoclopramide |
| Anxiety | Lorazepam 0.5–1 mg |

- Stop non-comfort meds, labs, vitals, telemetry, fingersticks.
- Liberalize visiting, chaplain, music, mouth care.
- Hospice referral if prognosis ≤6 months and goals are comfort.

## ✍️ Pronouncing death

1. Confirm identity (wristband). Note the time you were called.
2. Observe for **1 minute**: no response to verbal/tactile stimulus.
3. **No pupillary light reflex** (fixed, dilated).
4. Auscultate **heart 1 minute** — no heart sounds; no carotid pulse.
5. Auscultate **lungs 1 minute** — no breath sounds.
6. Declare: time of death.
7. Notify attending, family (by phone with care), **organ procurement organization** (required within 1 h), medical examiner if indicated (trauma, unexpected, suspicious, within 24 h of admission depending on state).
8. Document: "Called to see patient for … Exam: … Pronounced dead at HH:MM. Family notified. Attending notified. OPO notified."

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Dying patient, gurgling breathing, family upset | Glycopyrrolate + reassurance |
| Patient with capacity refuses intubation | Respect the decision (autonomy) |
| Surrogate decision standard | Substituted judgment (what the patient would want) |
| Opioid at end of life hastening death? | Double effect — intent is relief; appropriate titration is ethical |
