:::bottom
- **Transfuse at Hb <7 g/dL** for most hospitalized adults (AABB 2023). **<8** for cardiac/orthopedic surgery or existing CVD.
- **Acute MI with anemia:** a more liberal strategy (Hb <10) may be reasonable (MINT trend toward benefit).
- **One unit at a time**, then reassess. Each unit raises Hb ~1 g/dL.
- Workup = **MCV + reticulocyte count** → then targeted labs. Send iron studies **before** transfusing if possible.
- **Iron deficiency** = ferritin <30 (or <100 with inflammation, CKD, HF) → find the source (GI workup in men and postmenopausal women).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Anemia = **too little production** (low retics) or **too much loss/destruction** (high retics).
- O₂ delivery = cardiac output × Hb × SaO₂. Healthy hearts compensate by raising output; **ischemic hearts cannot** → why CAD patients may need a higher threshold.
- Hb drops slowly in chronic anemia → patients tolerate very low values; acute bleeds look worse at the same Hb.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| MCV | Low retics (production) | High retics (loss/destruction) |
|---|---|---|
| **Low (<80)** | **Iron deficiency**, thalassemia, anemia of inflammation (some), sideroblastic, lead | — |
| **Normal** | Anemia of inflammation/CKD, early iron deficiency, marrow failure, mixed | **Acute bleed, hemolysis** |
| **High (>100)** | **B12/folate**, alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroid, MDS, drugs (MTX, hydroxyurea, AZT) | Reticulocytosis itself raises MCV |

| Hemolysis labs | Pattern |
|---|---|
| LDH ↑, indirect bilirubin ↑, **haptoglobin ↓**, retics ↑ | Hemolysis |
| Smear: schistocytes | **MAHA** → TTP/HUS/DIC/valve → urgent (PLASMIC score, ADAMTS13) |
| Smear: spherocytes + **DAT positive** | Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia |
| Bite cells, Heinz bodies | G6PD (after drug, infection) |

| Iron studies | Iron deficiency | Inflammation |
|---|---|---|
| Ferritin | **Low** (<30) | Normal/high |
| TSAT | Low (<20%) | Low |
| TIBC | High | Low |

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Transfusion
| Situation | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Most hospitalized adults, ICU | **Hb <7** |
| Cardiac surgery, orthopedic surgery, pre-existing CVD | Hb <7.5–8 |
| Acute MI | Consider Hb <10 (MINT) — individualize |
| Active bleeding, hemorrhagic shock | Clinical, not a number (massive transfusion protocol) |
| Oncology / hematologic malignancy | Hb <7 (often 7–8) |

- Watch **TACO** (volume overload: give slowly, diurese between units in HF) and **TRALI** (hypoxemia within 6 h, non-cardiogenic).
- Febrile non-hemolytic reaction → stop, rule out hemolysis.
=== Iron deficiency
- **Oral:** ferrous sulfate 325 mg (65 mg elemental) **once daily or every other day** (better absorption, fewer GI effects).
- **IV iron** if: intolerance, malabsorption (celiac, bariatric), CKD, HFrEF, ongoing loss, need for fast repletion (ferric carboxymaltose, ferric derisomaltose, iron sucrose).
- **Find the source:** EGD + colonoscopy (men, postmenopausal women); celiac serology; menstrual history.
=== B12 / folate
- B12 deficiency: **IM cyanocobalamin 1000 mcg** daily ×1 week → weekly → monthly, or high-dose oral 1000–2000 mcg daily (works even without intrinsic factor).
- Check B12 **before** giving folate (folate can mask B12 → neuro damage).
- Methylmalonic acid ↑ = B12; homocysteine ↑ in both.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Transfusing "to 8" every morning in stable patients.
- Iron studies drawn after transfusion (useless).
- Ferritin 60 called "normal" in a CKD/HF patient.
- Missing MAHA → TTP (look at the smear!).

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Hb 6.8, ICU sepsis, stable | Transfuse 1 unit |
| Hb 7.4, no symptoms, no CAD | No transfusion |
| Microcytic, ferritin 8, man 60 y | Iron deficiency → EGD + colonoscopy |
| Schistocytes, platelets 20 k, fever, AMS | TTP → plasma exchange, caplacizumab |
| Hypoxemia 2 h after transfusion, no volume overload | TRALI |
| Macrocytosis + neuropathy + vitiligo | Pernicious anemia (B12) |
