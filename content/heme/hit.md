:::bottom
- **Calculate 4Ts** before ordering tests. **0–3 = low** → HIT very unlikely, don't send the test, keep heparin.
- **4Ts ≥4 → stop ALL heparin** (flushes, LMWH, heparin-coated lines) **and start a non-heparin anticoagulant** — even before results.
- **Argatroban** (liver OK, kidney failure OK), bivalirudin, **fondaparinux**, or a **DOAC** (stable patients).
- **No platelet transfusions** (unless bleeding). **No warfarin until platelets ≥150 k**; overlap ≥5 days.
- HIT is **pro-thrombotic** — check leg Dopplers. Treat 3 months if thrombosis; 4 weeks–3 months if isolated HIT.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Heparin binds **platelet factor 4 (PF4)** → new shape → body makes **IgG antibodies** against PF4–heparin.
- Antibody complexes **activate platelets** via FcγRIIa → platelets clump (count falls) and release procoagulants → **thrombosis** (venous > arterial).
- Platelets low **but clotting** — the paradox. Bleeding is rare.
- Timing: antibodies need **5–10 days** to form (or <1 day if heparin in last 30–100 days — "rapid onset").

## 🔎 Diagnosis — 4Ts

| | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T**hrombocytopenia | Fall >50% and nadir ≥20 k | Fall 30–50% or nadir 10–19 k | <30% or nadir <10 k |
| **T**iming | Day 5–10, or ≤1 day with recent heparin (≤30 d) | >day 10, unclear, or ≤1 day with heparin 30–100 d ago | ≤4 days, no recent heparin |
| **T**hrombosis | New thrombosis, skin necrosis, systemic reaction after bolus | Progressive/recurrent/suspected | None |
| o**T**her cause | None | Possible | Definite (sepsis, DIC, drugs, bleed) |

**0–3 low · 4–5 intermediate · 6–8 high.**

- **Intermediate/high → PF4 immunoassay (ELISA).** High OD (>1.0–2.0) = more likely.
- **Serotonin release assay (SRA)** confirms (functional, days).
- Lower-extremity Doppler ultrasound in all confirmed/suspected HIT (silent DVT is common).

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Anticoagulants
| Drug | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Argatroban** | IV; **liver clearance** → good in renal failure | Raises INR (complicates warfarin switch); reduce in liver disease |
| Bivalirudin | IV; short half-life; renal adjust | Cardiac procedures |
| **Fondaparinux** | SC daily, weight-based | Avoid CrCl <30 |
| **Rivaroxaban / apixaban** | Stable patients, no high bleeding risk | Use VTE treatment doses |
=== Duration & transition
- HIT **with thrombosis (HITT): 3 months**.
- **Isolated HIT:** at least until platelets recover; many treat 4 weeks–3 months (high early thrombosis risk).
- Warfarin only after platelets ≥150 k; start low, **overlap ≥5 days** and until INR therapeutic 24 h (risk of venous limb gangrene from protein C drop).
=== Future heparin
- Avoid heparin in the future when possible; document allergy.
- Cardiac surgery with remote HIT (antibody negative) → brief intra-op heparin is acceptable.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Sending PF4 ELISA on everyone with low platelets (false positives → harm).
- Stopping heparin **without** starting another anticoagulant.
- Forgetting heparin flushes and coated catheters.
- Transfusing platelets "because count is low."

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Day 7 of heparin, platelets 220 → 90 k, new DVT | HIT → stop heparin, start argatroban |
| Platelets fall day 2 of heparin, no prior exposure | Not HIT (too early) |
| HIT + CrCl 15 | Argatroban |
| HIT, platelets 60 k, on argatroban, start warfarin? | Wait until ≥150 k |
| Skin necrosis at LMWH injection site | HIT |
