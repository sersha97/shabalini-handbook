:::bottom
- **Severity decides site of care:** PSI or CURB-65 + judgment; IDSA/ATS **severe criteria** for ICU.
- **Non-severe inpatient:** **ceftriaxone + azithromycin** (or doxycycline), or a respiratory fluoroquinolone alone.
- **Cover MRSA/Pseudomonas only if prior isolate** — or recent hospitalization + IV antibiotics **and** local risk. Get cultures + **nasal MRSA PCR** and de-escalate.
- **Severe CAP → add hydrocortisone** (ATS 2025). **No steroids for non-severe CAP.**
- **Short courses:** stable by day 3 → 3–5 days total is enough for non-severe CAP. Longer for severe, *S. aureus*, Pseudomonas, empyema.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Micro-aspiration of oropharyngeal flora (or inhaled droplets) beats the lung's defenses → alveolar inflammation → exudate fills alveoli → **V/Q mismatch and shunt** → hypoxemia.
- Common bugs: *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*, atypicals (*Mycoplasma*, *Chlamydophila*, *Legionella*), **viruses** (influenza, RSV, SARS-CoV-2, hMPV). *S. aureus* after influenza.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

- New infiltrate + symptoms (fever, cough, dyspnea, pleuritic pain, crackles).
- **Lung ultrasound** is an acceptable alternative to CXR where expertise exists (ATS 2025).
- **Inpatients:** blood cultures + sputum culture if severe or MRSA/PsA risk; **Legionella + pneumococcal urine antigens** if severe; respiratory viral PCR; nasal MRSA PCR if MRSA coverage started.
- Procalcitonin: does **not** decide whether to start antibiotics.

**Severe CAP (IDSA/ATS):** 1 major (septic shock on pressors, or mechanical ventilation) **or ≥3 minor** (RR ≥30, P/F ≤250, multilobar, confusion, BUN ≥20, WBC <4 k, platelets <100 k, T <36 °C, hypotension needing fluids).

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Empiric antibiotics
| Setting | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Ward, non-severe | **Ceftriaxone 1–2 g daily + azithromycin 500 mg daily** (or doxycycline 100 mg BID) · or levofloxacin 750 mg / moxifloxacin 400 mg |
| Severe / ICU | β-lactam + **azithromycin** (preferred) or + respiratory FQ |
| Prior MRSA (resp) | Add **vancomycin** or linezolid → stop if nasal PCR & cultures negative |
| Prior Pseudomonas (resp) | **Cefepime / pip-tazo / meropenem** |
| Aspiration | **No routine anaerobic coverage** unless abscess or empyema |
| Influenza positive | **Oseltamivir** too |
=== Steroids (ATS 2025)
- **Severe CAP:** hydrocortisone **200 mg/day** (e.g., 50 mg IV q6h or infusion) for 4–7 days, taper by response (CAPE COD regimen).
- **Non-severe CAP:** do **not** give systemic steroids (strong recommendation).
- Avoid in influenza pneumonia; caution in aspergillosis risk.
=== Duration & switch
- Switch to PO when improving, afebrile, eating, hemodynamically stable.
- **Non-severe, clinically stable by day 3** → minimum 3 days; ~5 days total is acceptable.
- **Severe CAP, MRSA, Pseudomonas, empyema, abscess** → individualize (≥7 days).
- **Positive viral test, hospitalized** → ATS 2025 suggests still giving antibacterial therapy (co-infection is hard to exclude); reassess early and stop if the bacterial workup is negative and the patient improves. Outpatients with a virus and no major comorbidity can often skip antibiotics.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Reflex "HCAP" vancomycin + pip-tazo (HCAP no longer exists).
- Missing **empyema** in a slow responder → ultrasound/CT, tap.
- 10–14 day courses "to be safe."
- Not checking **QTc** with azithro/FQ combos.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| CAP, ward, no MRSA/PsA risk | Ceftriaxone + azithromycin |
| Severe CAP on pressors | β-lactam + macrolide + hydrocortisone |
| Necrotizing pneumonia after influenza | *S. aureus* (MRSA) → vancomycin/linezolid |
| Hyponatremia, diarrhea, high fever, confusion | *Legionella* → urine antigen, azithro/levofloxacin |
| Still febrile day 4, dull base | Parapneumonic effusion → tap |
