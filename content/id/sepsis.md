:::bottom
- **Screen with NEWS2, MEWS or SIRS** — not qSOFA alone (SSC 2026).
- **Shock or probable sepsis → cultures, then antibiotics within 1 hour.** Possible sepsis without shock → rapid workup, antibiotics **within 3 hours** if infection still likely.
- **Hypoperfusion → ≥30 mL/kg balanced crystalloid** in the first 3 hours (ideal/adjusted weight if BMI >30). Guide more fluid with dynamic tests.
- **Norepinephrine** for MAP ≥65 (**60–65 if age ≥65**). Peripheral start is OK.
- Still on pressors → **hydrocortisone 200 mg/day**.
- **Find and control the source.** De-escalate as soon as cultures return.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- **Sepsis-3:** life-threatening **organ dysfunction** (SOFA rise ≥2) from a **dysregulated host response** to infection.
- **Septic shock:** sepsis + **vasopressors for MAP ≥65** + **lactate >2** despite fluids.
- Vasodilation (NO), capillary leak, myocardial depression and microcirculatory failure → tissues starve even with "OK" BP.
- Lactate rises from **β₂-adrenergic glycolysis** as much as from hypoxia — still a strong risk marker.

:::new SSC 2026 — what changed
- Screening: **NEWS/NEWS2/MEWS/SIRS over qSOFA** (strong).
- **Balanced crystalloids** over saline; **crystalloids alone** over adding albumin (albumin reasonable after large volumes or in cirrhosis).
- **Prolonged-infusion β-lactams** after the loading dose (strong).
- **De-escalation** once susceptibilities return (strong).
- **No routine anaerobic coverage** unless risk factors (abdominal/pelvic source, necrotizing infection, head–neck, CNS abscess).
- MAP **>65** initial target; **60–65 in age ≥65**. Non-invasive BP acceptable at low pressor doses.
- After resuscitation, **active fluid removal** is suggested.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis & workup

- **Two sets of blood cultures** before antibiotics (do not delay abx >45 min for them).
- Lactate (repeat in 2–4 h if >2), CBC, BMP, LFTs, coags, UA/urine culture, CXR, procalcitonin (helps stopping, not starting).
- Source imaging: CT abdomen, ultrasound RUQ/kidneys, line/skin check.
- **POCUS:** IVC, LV/RV function, B-lines, free fluid.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Antibiotics
| Situation | Empiric choice (adjust to local antibiogram) |
|---|---|
| Unknown source, community | **Ceftriaxone** ± vancomycin (MRSA risk) |
| Hospital-acquired / healthcare risk | **Cefepime** or **pip-tazo** + **vancomycin** |
| Prior ESBL / recent carbapenem-sensitive only | **Meropenem** |
| Abdominal source | Pip-tazo (or ceftriaxone + metronidazole) |
| Neutropenic | Cefepime / pip-tazo / meropenem (+ vanc if indicated) |
| Toxic shock / necrotizing | Add **clindamycin** (or linezolid) for toxin suppression |

Narrow within 48–72 h. Typical duration **7 days** for most sources with good source control.
=== Fluids & pressors
- 30 mL/kg balanced crystalloid (LR / Plasma-Lyte) in 3 h when hypoperfused.
- Then **fluid only if responsive**: passive leg raise, pulse-pressure variation, cap refill time.
- **Norepinephrine** first (0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min).
- Add **vasopressin 0.03 U/min** when NE ~0.25 mcg/kg/min.
- Add **epinephrine** if MAP still low. **Dobutamine** if low cardiac output.
- Arterial line if pressor needs are rising or multiple agents.
=== Steroids & extras
- **Hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h** (± fludrocortisone 50 mcg daily) for ongoing pressor need. Stop when off pressors.
- Glucose 140–180.
- VTE and stress-ulcer prophylaxis (if risk factors).
- Transfuse at **Hb <7**.
- No vitamin C. Bicarbonate only if pH ≤7.2 with AKI.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Waiting for the lactate or CT before antibiotics in shock.
- 6 L of fluid with no reassessment → edema, ARDS, AKI.
- "Pan-cultures" without looking for a drainable source.
- Staying broad after cultures return.
- Rate-controlling compensatory tachycardia (AF in sepsis).
