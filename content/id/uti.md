:::bottom
- **Asymptomatic bacteriuria → do not treat** (except pregnancy and before urologic procedures with mucosal injury). Cloudy/smelly urine ≠ UTI.
- **IDSA 2025:** infection **confined to the bladder** (afebrile) = **uncomplicated** in women *and men*. Infection **beyond the bladder** (fever, flank pain, bacteremia, CAUTI with systemic signs, prostatitis) = **complicated**.
- **cUTI empiric (no sepsis):** 3rd/4th-gen cephalosporin, pip-tazo or FQ. **Sepsis:** broaden (carbapenem if ESBL risk).
- **Switch to oral** when improving. **Duration 7 days** (5–7 for FQ), also with gram-negative bacteremia if improving.
- **Obstruction (stone) + infection = urology now** (decompression).
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Gut flora (*E. coli* ~75%) colonize the periurethral area → ascend to bladder → kidneys.
- Risk: female anatomy, sex, catheters, obstruction (stones, BPH), diabetes, pregnancy, urinary retention.
- **Catheters** grow a biofilm in days → bacteriuria is almost universal by 30 days → **culture only if symptoms**.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Picture | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Cystitis** | Dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain — **no fever** |
| **Complicated UTI** | Fever, chills, flank pain/CVA tenderness, rigors, bacteremia, sepsis, delirium **with** no other source |
| **CAUTI** | Catheter (or removed ≤48 h) + symptoms + ≥10³ CFU/mL; no other source |
| **ASB** | ≥10⁵ CFU/mL, no symptoms |

- **UA + culture before antibiotics.** Pyuria supports but does not prove UTI; **no pyuria → UTI unlikely**.
- Blood cultures if febrile/septic.
- **Imaging** (CT or US) if sepsis, no improvement in 48–72 h, stone history, or suspected abscess/obstruction.
- Confused older patient without urinary symptoms or fever → **look for other causes first**.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Cystitis (afebrile)
| Drug | Course |
|---|---|
| Nitrofurantoin 100 mg BID | 5 days (not if CrCl <30 or suspected pyelo) |
| TMP-SMX DS BID | 3 days (if resistance <20%) |
| Fosfomycin 3 g | Single dose |
| Cephalexin / cefpodoxime | 5–7 days |

Men with afebrile cystitis: same drugs; often **7 days**.
=== Complicated UTI / pyelo
1. **Empiric:** ceftriaxone 1–2 g daily, cefepime, pip-tazo, or FQ. Avoid drugs that failed before (prior resistant isolate).
2. **Sepsis or ESBL risk** → meropenem/ertapenem.
3. **Narrow** to culture results.
4. **IV → PO** when improving (e.g., FQ, TMP-SMX, oral β-lactam with high dose).
5. **Duration:** **7 days** (FQ 5–7 days). Longer for abscess, prostatitis, slow response, undrained obstruction.
=== CAUTI
- **Remove or replace** the catheter (if in >2 weeks), then culture from the new catheter.
- 7 days if prompt response; 10–14 if delayed.
- Prevention: avoid catheters; remove daily review; bladder scan protocols.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Treating a positive culture in a delirious patient with no urinary symptoms or fever — then missing pneumonia.
- Nitrofurantoin for pyelonephritis (poor tissue levels).
- 14 days of IV ceftriaxone for a simple pyelo.
- Missing an **obstructed infected kidney** — needs drainage, not just antibiotics.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Nursing home, no symptoms, culture >10⁵ | Do not treat |
| Pregnant, ASB | Treat (cephalexin, nitrofurantoin) |
| Fever, flank pain, obstructing stone on CT | Antibiotics + urgent decompression (stent/nephrostomy) |
| Man, dysuria, afebrile | Cystitis — uncomplicated under IDSA 2025 |
| Pyelo, improving on ceftriaxone, cultures sensitive | Oral switch, 7 days total |
