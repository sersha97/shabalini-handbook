:::bottom
- **Screen everyone with alcohol use: PAWSS ≥4 = high risk** of complicated withdrawal.
- **Symptom-triggered benzodiazepines** (CIWA-Ar ≥8–10) for most. CIWA needs a patient who can talk.
- **Severe / resistant / DTs → phenobarbital** (or escalating benzos) → ICU.
- **Thiamine before glucose.** Wernicke risk → **thiamine 200–500 mg IV TID**.
- Start **AUD treatment** before discharge (naltrexone, acamprosate, gabapentin) + linkage.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Chronic alcohol **boosts GABA-A** and **blocks NMDA**. The brain adapts: fewer GABA receptors, more NMDA receptors.
- Stop alcohol → **too little inhibition, too much excitation** → tremor, sympathetic surge, seizures, delirium.
- Each withdrawal episode makes the next one worse (**kindling**).
- Benzodiazepines and phenobarbital replace the missing GABA effect; phenobarbital also blocks glutamate.

## 🔎 Timeline

| Hours after last drink | Syndrome |
|---|---|
| 6–24 h | Minor withdrawal: tremor, anxiety, sweats, tachycardia, insomnia |
| 12–48 h | **Withdrawal seizures** (generalized, brief) |
| 12–48 h | Alcoholic hallucinosis (clear sensorium) |
| **48–96 h** | **Delirium tremens**: confusion, autonomic instability, fever — mortality 1–5% treated |

| PAWSS items (examples) |
|---|
| Prior withdrawal, prior seizures/DTs, prior alcohol rehab, blackouts, combined with downers, other drug use, BAC >200 on arrival, autonomic hyperactivity (HR >120, tremor, sweats) |

Rule out: hypoglycemia, infection (meningitis, pneumonia), head injury (subdural!), hepatic encephalopathy, other drug withdrawal/toxicity.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Benzodiazepines
- **Symptom-triggered** (CIWA ≥8–10): **lorazepam** 1–4 mg (liver disease, elderly) or **chlordiazepoxide** 25–100 mg / diazepam 10–20 mg (long-acting, smoother).
- Front-loading diazepam 10–20 mg IV q5–10 min for severe agitation.
- Escalating doses without response → **benzodiazepine-resistant** → phenobarbital, ICU.
=== Phenobarbital
- Single load **10 mg/kg IV (ideal body weight)** over 30 min (some protocols 10–15 mg/kg) — self-tapers (long half-life).
- Or as add-on 130–260 mg IV boluses.
- Watch sedation and breathing, especially combined with benzos.
=== Vitamins & supportive
- **Thiamine:** 100 mg daily if low risk; **200–500 mg IV TID × 3–5 days if Wernicke suspected** (confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia — often only one sign).
- Folate, multivitamin. **Replete K, Mg, Phos** (refeeding).
- Avoid antipsychotics alone (lower seizure threshold) — only as adjunct for hallucinations.
- Dexmedetomidine: adjunct for autonomic signs, does not prevent seizures.
=== AUD treatment
| Drug | Note |
|---|---|
| **Naltrexone** 50 mg daily / 380 mg IM monthly | Not with opioids or acute hepatitis/liver failure |
| **Acamprosate** 666 mg TID | Safe in liver disease; dose-reduce CrCl 30–50, avoid <30 |
| Gabapentin 300–600 mg TID | Also helps mild withdrawal/insomnia |
| Baclofen | Option in cirrhosis |
| Topiramate | Off-label |
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- CIWA scoring in a delirious, intubated or non-verbal patient.
- Giving glucose before thiamine.
- Missing a **subdural hematoma** or meningitis as the cause of "DTs."
- Discharging without AUD medication or follow-up.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Day 3 after admission: fever, tachycardia, confusion, hallucinations | Delirium tremens |
| Hallucinations, oriented, normal vitals, 24 h after last drink | Alcoholic hallucinosis |
| Confusion, ataxia, nystagmus | Wernicke → high-dose IV thiamine |
| Withdrawal + cirrhosis — which benzo? | Lorazepam (or oxazepam) |
| AUD + cirrhosis — which relapse drug? | Acamprosate or baclofen |
