:::bottom
- **Back or leg pain + any new saddle, bladder, bowel or sexual change** = suspected CES. **Emergency MRI**, ideally **within 4 h**.
- **Do not wait** for retention. A normal bladder scan or normal exam does **not** rule CES out. **The MRI decides.**
- Order **MRI lumbar spine**; **whole spine** if cancer, fever/IVDU or unclear level.
- **Call spine surgery when you order the MRI**, not after the read. Compression = **emergency decompression**.
- **Cause sets the extra step:** disc → surgery · cancer → **dexamethasone** + surgery/radiation · abscess → **cultures + antibiotics** + surgery · hematoma → **reverse anticoagulation** + surgery.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- The spinal cord ends at **L1–L2** (conus medullaris). Below that the canal holds only **nerve roots (L2–S5)** — the cauda equina, the "horse's tail."
- Something squeezes many roots at once → **leg pain/weakness** plus loss of the **S2–S4** roots → **saddle numbness, bladder, bowel and sexual dysfunction**.
- Roots are **peripheral nerves** → **lower motor neuron** signs: flaccid, areflexic, no Babinski.
- **Most common cause: large central disc herniation at L4–L5 or L5–S1.** Others: metastasis, epidural abscess, epidural hematoma, severe stenosis, trauma, post-surgery.
- **Rare** (~1–3 per 100,000/year), but missed CES = lifelong incontinence and sexual dysfunction. Many suspected cases are scanned to find one real one — **that is the correct trade.**

:::analogy
The spinal cord is the main power cable. At L1–L2 it splits into a bundle of extension cords (the horse's tail) that run down to the legs, bladder and bowel. A big disc bulge is a heavy box dropped on the whole bundle. The cords for the bladder and bowel (S2–S4) sit in the middle, so they get crushed first — and they are the slowest to recover.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

### Ask every patient with back pain (they will not tell you)

{{fig:ces-redflags}}

### Examine

- **Legs:** power, reflexes (**ankle jerk** = S1), sensation by dermatome. Look for **bilateral** findings.
- **Saddle sensation:** light touch + pinprick, perineum and perianal skin. Document it **side by side** each time.
- **Rectal exam:** anal tone, voluntary squeeze, perianal sensation. (Normal tone does **not** exclude early CES.)
- **Bladder scan post-void residual (PVR):** **>200 mL** raises concern; painless retention = late sign (CES-R). Low PVR does **not** rule out CES.

### Image

- **MRI lumbar spine, now** — ideally done **≤4 h** from request, 24/7. Do not delay for blood tests.
- **Whole-spine MRI with contrast** if known cancer, fever/bacteremia/IVDU (abscess), or level unclear.
- MRI not possible (pacemaker not MRI-conditional, metal) → **CT myelogram**.
- **Labs** (do not delay MRI): CBC, ESR/CRP, blood cultures if febrile, coags/platelets if on anticoagulants.

### Cord vs conus vs cauda — where is the lesion?

| | Cord compression (above L1) | Conus medullaris (L1–L2) | Cauda equina (below L2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neuron type | **UMN** | **Mixed** UMN + LMN | **LMN** |
| Pain | Back pain, band-like | Mild, back | **Severe radicular**, often both legs |
| Onset | Variable | Sudden, **symmetric** | Gradual or sudden, often **asymmetric** |
| Reflexes | **Brisk**, Babinski + | Knee kept, **ankle lost**, ± Babinski | **Absent** (knee and/or ankle) |
| Weakness | Spastic, sensory level | Mild, symmetric | Flaccid, can be marked |
| Bladder/bowel | Late | **Early** and severe | Often later |
| Saddle | — | Symmetric | Asymmetric |

:::pearl
In real life the lines blur. The exam tells you **how urgent**; the **MRI** tells you **where and why**. If unsure of the level, scan the whole spine.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Back/leg pain + new saddle, bladder, bowel or sexual symptom (or bilateral leg symptoms)?
Y: Suspected CES. Full neuro + saddle + rectal exam + PVR. Order **emergency MRI** and **page spine surgery now**.
N: Ordinary back pain or one-sided sciatica → treat, and **safety-net**: "come back now if numb saddle, bladder or bowel change."
Q: Compression on MRI that matches the symptoms?
Y: **Emergency decompression** (laminectomy ± discectomy). Earlier is better — aim to operate before CES-R.
N: Look for mimics (below). Re-examine. Escalate if symptoms progress.
Q: Known or likely cancer?
Y: **Dexamethasone 10 mg IV**, then ~16 mg/day divided. Oncology + radiation oncology + spine surgery (surgery if unstable spine or single level).
Q: Fever, bacteremia, IVDU, recent spine procedure?
Y: Think **epidural abscess**: blood cultures → **vancomycin + ceftriaxone or cefepime** (no delay for MRI). Surgery for neuro deficit.
Q: On anticoagulants, after LP/epidural/spine surgery?
Y: Think **epidural hematoma**: hold and **reverse anticoagulation**, platelets if low → urgent evacuation.
:::

:::tabs
=== While you wait for MRI/OR
- **Keep NPO** (likely OR).
- **Bladder:** if PVR high or retention → **Foley catheter**. Record PVR and time.
- Pain control: acetaminophen, opioids as needed. Avoid heavy sedation that masks the neuro exam.
- **Hold anticoagulation** if surgery likely; check platelets and INR.
- Serial neuro and saddle exams; document time stamps.
=== After surgery
- Bladder: voiding trials, intermittent catheterization if needed; urology follow-up (urodynamics).
- Bowel regimen; PT/OT; DVT prophylaxis when surgeon agrees.
- Recovery of bladder, bowel and sexual function can take **months to years**. Ask about mood, pain and sexual health.
- Pelvic floor physiotherapy; refer to CES support resources.
=== Mimics (MRI normal)
- Opioid- or anticholinergic-induced **urinary retention**, BPH, constipation.
- Pain limiting voiding; post-op retention.
- Peripheral neuropathy, Guillain-Barré (ascending, areflexic — check NIF/FVC), transverse myelitis.
- Functional neurological disorder (diagnosis of exclusion, after imaging).
:::

:::dose
- **Dexamethasone** (malignant compression): 10 mg IV once, then ~16 mg/day PO/IV divided (e.g., 4 mg q6h) with PPI and glucose checks. **Not** for disc herniation.
- **Vancomycin** (epidural abscess): weight-based, AUC-guided + **ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h** (CNS dose) or **cefepime 2 g IV q8h**. Check dose with pharmacy.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **Waiting for retention or incontinence.** These are **late** signs. Retention = CES-R = worse outcome.
- "Rectal tone normal, so no CES." Tone is often normal early.
- Not asking about saddle, bladder, bowel and sex because the patient "only has back pain."
- Blaming retention on **opioids** in a patient with new back pain — scan first.
- Ordering MRI "routine" or "AM" instead of **emergency**; not calling spine until after the read.
- Missing **epidural abscess** in a febrile IVDU patient or **hematoma** after an epidural on enoxaparin.
- Poor documentation: saddle exam, PVR and timeline matter for care and are often reviewed later.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Back pain, bilateral sciatica, new urinary hesitancy, perianal numbness | **Emergency MRI lumbar spine** + spine surgery consult |
| MRI: large central L4–L5 disc compressing cauda equina | **Urgent surgical decompression** |
| Breast cancer, back pain, leg weakness, retention | **Dexamethasone** + whole-spine MRI → radiation/surgery |
| IVDU, fever, back pain, new leg weakness | Epidural abscess → blood cultures, **vanc + ceftriaxone/cefepime**, MRI, surgery |
| On enoxaparin, epidural catheter removed, now leg numbness + retention | Epidural hematoma → MRI + emergent evacuation |
| Sudden symmetric saddle anesthesia, early incontinence, ankle reflex lost, Babinski + | **Conus medullaris** syndrome |
| Asymmetric radicular pain, areflexia, saddle numbness later | **Cauda equina** syndrome |
| Old man, opioids post-op, retention, no back pain, normal neuro | Drug-induced retention (but low threshold to image if any neuro sign) |
