:::bottom
- **Status epilepticus = seizure ≥5 minutes** or repeated seizures without return to baseline.
- **0–5 min:** ABCs, glucose, IV access. **5–20 min: benzodiazepine — full dose** (lorazepam 4 mg IV, repeat once; or **midazolam 10 mg IM**).
- **20–40 min: second-line** — **levetiracetam 60 mg/kg** (max 4500 mg) **or fosphenytoin 20 PE/kg** or valproate 40 mg/kg (ESETT: equal).
- Still seizing → **intubate**, continuous infusion (propofol/midazolam), **continuous EEG**.
- Not waking up after the seizure stops → think **non-convulsive status** → EEG.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Seizure = synchronized excessive neuronal firing. Normally GABA inhibition stops it.
- In prolonged seizures, **GABA-A receptors are pulled inside the cell** and NMDA receptors increase → **benzodiazepines lose power with time** → treat early and with full doses.
- Prolonged seizures injure neurons (excitotoxicity) + cause hyperthermia, rhabdo, lactic acidosis, aspiration.

## 🔎 Diagnosis

| Provoked causes — check |
|---|
| **Glucose** (hypo/hyper) · Na, Ca, Mg · alcohol/benzodiazepine **withdrawal** · drugs (bupropion, tramadol, cefepime, carbapenems, TCA, isoniazid) · infection (meningitis/encephalitis) · stroke, bleed, tumor · eclampsia · hypoxia · AED non-adherence (levels) |

- Labs: glucose, BMP, Ca, Mg, Phos, CK, lactate (transient high), tox, AED levels, **pregnancy test**.
- **Head CT** for new seizure or trauma; MRI later.
- **EEG**; LP if fever, immunosuppressed, or not waking.
- Lactate and prolactin (10–20 min post-ictal) support epileptic vs psychogenic but are not definitive.

| Seizure vs syncope | Seizure | Syncope |
|---|---|---|
| Duration of LOC | 1–3 min | Seconds |
| Post-event confusion | Minutes–hours | Brief |
| Tongue bite | Lateral | Rare / tip |
| Jerks | Before fall, long | Few, brief, after fall |

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: 0–5 min
Y: ABC, O₂, glucose (thiamine 100–500 mg IV before glucose if malnourished/alcohol), IV ×2, labs.
Q: 5–20 min — benzodiazepine
Y: Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (4 mg) IV, repeat in 5 min · or midazolam 10 mg IM (>40 kg) · or diazepam 0.15–0.2 mg/kg IV.
Q: 20–40 min — second line (pick one)
Y: Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (max 4500) · fosphenytoin 20 PE/kg (max 1500 PE; watch BP/QRS) · valproate 40 mg/kg (max 3000; avoid in liver disease, pregnancy, urea-cycle disorders)
Q: >40 min — refractory
Y: Intubate · propofol or midazolam infusion · continuous EEG · ICU / neurology.
:::

:::tabs
=== Special causes
| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Eclampsia | **Magnesium sulfate 4–6 g IV** → 1–2 g/h; deliver |
| Hyponatremia (seizing) | **3% saline 100–150 mL bolus** |
| Isoniazid toxicity | **Pyridoxine** (gram for gram) |
| Alcohol withdrawal | Benzodiazepines / phenobarbital |
| Hypoglycemia | Dextrose |
=== First unprovoked seizure
- MRI brain + EEG. Recurrence risk ~35% at 5 years (higher with abnormal EEG/MRI, nocturnal seizure).
- Starting an AED lowers early recurrence; shared decision.
- **Driving:** counsel per **state law** (most US states: seizure-free 3–12 months). Document.
- Safety: no swimming alone, no heights, showers not baths.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- **Under-dosing** benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam 1 mg × several) → refractory status.
- Calling a confused post-ictal patient "just post-ictal" for hours → NCSE.
- Fosphenytoin without monitoring (hypotension, arrhythmia).
- Missing **cefepime neurotoxicity** in CKD.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Seizing 7 min, no IV | Midazolam 10 mg IM |
| Still seizing after 2 lorazepam doses | Levetiracetam / fosphenytoin / valproate |
| Pregnant 34 wk, seizure, BP 170/110 | Magnesium sulfate |
| TB patient on INH, refractory seizures | Pyridoxine |
| Alcohol use disorder, seizure, glucose 50 | Thiamine + dextrose |
