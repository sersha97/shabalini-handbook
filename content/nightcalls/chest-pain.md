:::bottom
- **"Get a 12-lead ECG now, I'm on my way."** Compare with the old one. Repeat in 15–30 min if pain continues.
- **Rule out the 6 killers:** ACS · aortic dissection · PE · tension pneumothorax · tamponade · esophageal rupture.
- **STEMI / posterior MI / new instability → cath lab activation.**
- Possible ACS → **aspirin 325 mg chewed** (no bleed/allergy), **nitro SL 0.4 mg** (not if SBP <90, RV infarct, PDE-5 in 24–48 h), troponin now and in 1–3 h.
- **Tearing pain to back, pulse/BP differential** → think **dissection** → CTA; **no** heparin/antiplatelets until excluded.
:::

## 📞 Phone orders

12-lead ECG · vitals incl. **BP both arms** · SpO₂ · troponin · CBC/BMP · portable CXR if dyspneic · O₂ only if SpO₂ <90%.

## 🔎 At the bedside

| Killer | Clues | First move |
|---|---|---|
| **ACS** | Pressure, exertional, diaphoresis, ECG changes | ASA, nitro, heparin, cardiology |
| **Aortic dissection** | Sudden **tearing** pain to back, **BP >20 mmHg difference** arms, neuro deficit, AR murmur, wide mediastinum | **CTA chest**; IV **esmolol** to HR <60 then **nicardipine** to SBP 100–120; surgery for type A |
| **PE** | Pleuritic, tachycardic, hypoxic, risk factors | Heparin (if low bleed risk) + CTPA |
| **Tension PTX** | Absent breath sounds, hypotension, tracheal shift, on ventilator/after line | **Needle decompression** (4th–5th ICS MAL) → chest tube |
| **Tamponade** | Hypotension, JVD, muffled, pulsus | POCUS → pericardiocentesis |
| **Esophageal rupture** | After vomiting/EGD, crepitus, pleural effusion | CT with oral contrast, NPO, antibiotics, surgery |

**Less deadly but common:** GERD/esophageal spasm, musculoskeletal (reproducible), pericarditis, pneumonia, anxiety, zoster (look at the skin!), biliary colic.

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: STEMI or equivalent on ECG?
Y: Activate cath lab · ASA · P2Y12 per cardiology · heparin.
Q: Dissection possible (tearing, back, pulse deficit)?
Y: CTA chest before any antithrombotic · esmolol → nicardipine · CT surgery.
Q: Hypoxic / tachycardic, PE risk?
Y: Wells → CTPA; heparin if high probability and low bleed risk.
Q: Ischemic pain, no STE?
Y: ASA, nitro, troponin 0/1–3 h, heparin if NSTEMI, cardiology in AM (or now if unstable).
N: Non-cardiac → treat (antacid, analgesia), document, reassess.
:::

:::warn Do not
- Give nitroglycerin to a hypotensive patient or one with RV infarct / recent sildenafil.
- Start heparin before you exclude dissection when the story fits.
- Blame "anxiety" before the ECG and troponin.
:::

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Post-op day 2, sudden chest pain, SpO₂ 85%, HR 125 | PE |
| Tearing pain, BP 170/90 R, 130/70 L | Aortic dissection → CTA, esmolol |
| Chest pain after forceful vomiting, crepitus | Boerhaave |
| Ventilated patient, sudden hypotension, high peak pressure, absent breath sounds R | Tension PTX → needle decompression |
