:::bottom
1. **pH** — acidemia or alkalemia?
2. **Primary process** — metabolic (HCO₃) or respiratory (PaCO₂)?
3. **Compensation** — is it appropriate? If not → second disorder.
4. **Anion gap** (correct for albumin).
5. **Delta–delta** if gap is high → hidden NAGMA or metabolic alkalosis?
6. **Osmolar gap** if high AG with no clear cause → toxic alcohol.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- pH depends on the ratio **HCO₃ ÷ PaCO₂** (Henderson–Hasselbalch).
- Lungs fix metabolic problems in **minutes–hours**; kidneys fix respiratory problems in **3–5 days**.
- **Anion gap** = unmeasured anions (albumin, lactate, ketones, toxins). Every 1 g/dL drop in albumin hides ~2.5 of gap.

## 🔎 Compensation rules

| Primary disorder | Expected compensation |
|---|---|
| Metabolic acidosis | **PaCO₂ = 1.5 × HCO₃ + 8 ± 2** (Winter's) |
| Metabolic alkalosis | PaCO₂ ≈ 0.7 × HCO₃ + 21 ± 2 |
| Acute respiratory acidosis | HCO₃ ↑ 1 per 10 PaCO₂ |
| Chronic respiratory acidosis | HCO₃ ↑ 3.5–4 per 10 |
| Acute respiratory alkalosis | HCO₃ ↓ 2 per 10 |
| Chronic respiratory alkalosis | HCO₃ ↓ 4–5 per 10 |

## 🧪 Causes

:::tabs
=== High AG acidosis
**GOLDMARK:** **G**lycols (ethylene, propylene) · **O**xoproline (acetaminophen + malnutrition) · **L**-lactate · **D**-lactate (short bowel) · **M**ethanol · **A**spirin · **R**enal failure · **K**etoacidosis (DKA, alcohol, starvation).

- Osmolar gap = measured − (2Na + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8 [+ EtOH/3.7]). **>10** → toxic alcohol (early); late it can be normal.
=== Normal AG acidosis
- **GI loss** (diarrhea, fistula) vs **renal** (RTA).
- **Urine AG** = UNa + UK − UCl: **negative = GI** (kidney excretes NH₄⁺ fine); **positive = RTA**.
- Also: large-volume **normal saline**, acetazolamide, ureteral diversion, early CKD.

| RTA | Serum K | Urine pH | Clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type 1 (distal) | Low | >5.5 | Stones, Sjögren |
| Type 2 (proximal) | Low | <5.5 (late) | Fanconi, myeloma, tenofovir |
| Type 4 | **High** | <5.5 | Diabetes, hypoaldosteronism, ACEi |
=== Metabolic alkalosis
- **Urine Cl <20 (saline-responsive):** vomiting, NG suction, prior diuretics, post-hypercapnia → NS + KCl.
- **Urine Cl >20 (saline-resistant):** hyperaldosteronism, Cushing, current diuretics, Bartter/Gitelman, severe hypoK → treat cause; acetazolamide if overloaded.
=== Respiratory
- **Acidosis:** opioids/sedatives, COPD/OHS, neuromuscular weakness, airway obstruction.
- **Alkalosis:** anxiety, pain, PE, pneumonia, sepsis (early), **salicylates**, pregnancy, liver failure.
:::

## 💡 Worked example

pH 7.25, PaCO₂ 24, HCO₃ 10, Na 140, Cl 100, albumin 4.

1. Acidemia. 2. Low HCO₃ → metabolic. 3. Winter's: 1.5×10 + 8 = 23 ± 2 → **appropriate**. 4. AG = 140 − 110 = **30** (high). 5. ΔAG 18 ÷ ΔHCO₃ 14 = **1.3** → pure HAGMA. 6. Check lactate, ketones, osm gap.

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| pH 7.50, PaCO₂ 22, AG 20 | Respiratory alkalosis + HAGMA → salicylate |
| Vomiting, urine Cl 8 | Saline-responsive metabolic alkalosis |
| Diarrhea, urine AG −20 | GI bicarbonate loss |
| AG acidosis, osm gap 30, vision loss | Methanol → fomepizole, dialysis |
| Acetaminophen chronic use, malnourished, high AG | 5-oxoproline (pyroglutamic) acidosis |
