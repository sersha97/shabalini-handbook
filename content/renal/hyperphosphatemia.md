:::bottom
- **Phos >4.5 mg/dL.** Most common cause: **kidney failure** (AKI or CKD).
- **First: is it real?** Hemolyzed sample, paraprotein, high lipids or bilirubin, heparin/tPA in the line can fake it.
- **Acute and high** (TLS, rhabdo, Fleet enema) → the danger is **low calcium** and **Ca-P crystals** in the kidney.
- **Acute:** stop the source, **IV fluids** if kidneys and heart allow, **dialysis** if severe with AKI or symptomatic low Ca.
- **Do not give calcium** for **asymptomatic** low Ca when phos is very high. **Give it** for tetany, seizures, arrhythmia or long QT.
- **Chronic CKD:** cut **phosphate additives**, give a **binder WITH meals**, limit calcium-based binders.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- We eat **~1000–1400 mg** of phosphate a day. The gut absorbs most of it; the **kidney** removes the same amount.
- When GFR falls below **~30**, the kidney cannot keep up (even with high **FGF23** and **PTH** pushing it out) → phosphate rises.
- High phosphate **binds calcium** → **low ionized Ca** (tetany, long QT, seizures) and **Ca-P crystals** in vessels, skin, heart valves and **kidney tubules**.
- In CKD, chronic high phos → **high PTH**, **vascular calcification**, and higher mortality.

{{fig:phos-seesaw}}

:::analogy
Calcium and phosphate are like **salt and water in a pot**. Add too much of either and crystals **fall out** — in arteries, in the skin (calciphylaxis) and in the kidney tubules (acute phosphate nephropathy).
:::

### Symptoms

- Most come from **low calcium**: perioral numbness, cramps, **tetany**, Chvostek/Trousseau, **prolonged QT**, seizures, hypotension.
- **Acute kidney injury** (crystals in tubules) — TLS, oral sodium phosphate bowel prep.
- **Chronic:** itching, vascular and valve calcification, **calciphylaxis** (painful necrotic skin lesions), renal osteodystrophy.

## 🔎 Causes

{{fig:hyperphos-4b}}

:::warn Phosphate "bombs" — think before you order
- **Fleet (sodium phosphate) enema** in old, CKD, dehydrated or constipated/obstructed patients → phos can go **>20 mg/dL**, severe low Ca, death.
- **Oral sodium phosphate bowel prep** → **acute phosphate nephropathy** (FDA boxed warning). Use PEG prep in CKD, elderly, ACEi/ARB/diuretic users.
- **IV phosphate** in a patient with a low GFR.
:::

## 🧪 Diagnosis

- **Repeat** if unexpected — non-hemolyzed, no line contamination.
- **Labs:** BMP (**creatinine**), **Ca** (ionized if possible), Mg, K, **uric acid**, LDH, **CK**, glucose, ABG/lactate if acidotic.
- **ECG** — QTc.
- Chronic: **PTH**, 25-OH vitamin D, alkaline phosphatase.
- High phos + **low PTH** + normal creatinine → **hypoparathyroidism** (often after neck surgery).

:::pearl Is the creatinine normal? Then ask "load or shift?"
Normal kidneys can clear huge amounts. If phos is high with a **normal GFR**, someone **gave** it (enema, IV, vitamin D), **cells broke** (TLS, rhabdo, hemolysis), **acidosis shifted** it out, or the **PTH is missing**.
:::

## 💊 Management

:::flow
Q: Is it real? (repeat if hemolyzed, paraprotein, line draw)
Y: Continue.
N: Pseudohyperphosphatemia — no treatment.
Q: Acute and severe (TLS, rhabdo, enema), or symptomatic low calcium?
Y: Stop the source · IV fluids (if no volume overload) · binders with any food · call nephrology.
Q: Severe (often >10–12 mg/dL) with AKI/oliguria, or symptomatic low Ca despite treatment?
Y: Dialysis (HD; CRRT in TLS often needed for rebound).
Q: Chronic (CKD)?
Y: Diet (cut additives) + binder with meals + adequate dialysis.
:::

:::tabs
=== Acute
- **Stop phosphate sources:** enemas, IV phos, phosphate-containing TPN/IV fluids, vitamin D.
- **IV fluids** (isotonic) → more renal excretion. Only if kidneys work and no volume overload. It can lower Ca further — watch.
- **Treat the cause:** TLS bundle (fluids, **rasburicase** for urate), rhabdo (fluids), acidosis (insulin in DKA shifts phos back in).
- **Binders** with any oral intake (sevelamer is safe in AKI).
- **Calcium:** only for **symptoms** (tetany, seizure, arrhythmia, long QT) → **calcium gluconate 1–2 g IV**. Asymptomatic low Ca → **do not** (more crystals).
- **Dialysis:** severe hyperphos with AKI, oliguria, or symptomatic low Ca. In TLS, phos **rebounds** → frequent or continuous therapy.
=== CKD — targets & diet
- **KDIGO 2017:** lower **elevated** phos **toward normal**. Avoid high calcium. No strict Ca × P product target any more.
- **Diet (with a renal dietitian):** the enemy is **phosphate additives** — almost **100%** absorbed (vs ~40–60% from plants).
    - Avoid: **dark colas**, processed/deli meats, fast food, processed cheese, many frozen and "enhanced" meats. Look for **"PHOS"** on the label (sodium phosphate, phosphoric acid…).
    - Plant protein is better than processed meat. Do not starve protein in dialysis patients.
- **Dialysis:** longer or more frequent sessions remove more phosphate.
- Treat **high PTH** (calcitriol/analog, cinacalcet) together with phos — CKD-MBD team.
=== Binders
**All binders work only if taken WITH meals.** Start dose with each meal (TID), titrate by the phos level.

| Binder | Start dose | Good | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium acetate** (667 mg cap) | **2 caps TID** with meals | Cheap, works | High Ca, calcification — limit elemental Ca ≤1500 mg/day |
| **Calcium carbonate** | 500 mg–1 g Ca TID | Cheap, OTC | Same; needs stomach acid (PPI ↓) |
| **Sevelamer carbonate** (800 mg) | **800–1600 mg TID** | No Ca, no metal, lowers LDL | Pill burden, GI upset; binds some drugs |
| **Lanthanum** (chewable 500–1000 mg) | **500 mg TID** | Strong, fewer pills | **Must chew**; GI upset; shows on X-ray |
| **Ferric citrate** (1 g tab) | **2 tabs TID** | Also gives **iron** | Dark stools, diarrhea, iron overload |
| **Sucroferric oxyhydroxide** (500 mg chewable) | **500 mg TID** | Low pill burden | Dark stools, diarrhea |
| **Tenapanor** (not a binder) | **30 mg BID** before breakfast/dinner | Blocks gut absorption (NHE3); **add-on** for dialysis | **Diarrhea** |
| ~~Aluminum hydroxide~~ | Short term only | Very strong | Aluminum toxicity — avoid long term |
:::

:::mnemonic Binders: "Meet the meat"
The binder must **meet the food** in the gut. A binder taken at bedtime binds nothing. **Skip the meal → skip the binder.** Snacks with protein → a binder dose too.
:::

:::dose
Doses are typical adult starting doses. Titrate to the phos level, and check Ca and PTH. Acute severe hyperphosphatemia needs nephrology.
:::

## 🏥 Real-life ward tips

- **"Phos 9, patient looks fine, Cr normal"** → check for a **hemolyzed sample** or **paraprotein** (myeloma) before you treat.
- **Never order a Fleet enema** for a patient with CKD, AKI, ileus or an obstruction. Use a PEG or mineral-oil option.
- **Dialysis patient admitted:** **continue the home binder with meals** — and **hold it when NPO**. Check the binder is on the MAR **with meals**, not "TID" at random times.
- **TLS:** watch phos, K, Ca, uric acid, creatinine **every 6–8 h** for the first 24–72 h after chemo starts.
- **Low Ca + high phos in AKI:** correct only if **symptomatic**. Many "low" Ca are low albumin — check **ionized Ca**.
- **Calcium acetate + calcium carbonate + vitamin D** in one patient = a recipe for **hypercalcemia** and calcification. Review the home list.
- **Rhabdo:** phos is high early, Ca **low early and high later** (recovery) — another reason not to give Ca unless symptomatic.

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- Treating a **pseudohyperphosphatemia** (paraprotein, hemolysis, heparin/tPA in sample).
- **IV calcium** for asymptomatic low Ca with very high phos → metastatic calcification.
- **Binder at bedtime** or while NPO.
- Fleet enema / oral sodium phosphate prep in CKD or elderly.
- Ignoring **phosphate additives** — "I don't eat dairy" but drinks 3 colas a day.
- Using **aluminum** binders long term.

| Elderly CKD patient, Fleet enema, phos 18, Ca 5.8, QTc 520 | Phosphate enema toxicity → IV Ca (symptomatic) + dialysis |
