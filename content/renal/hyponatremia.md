:::bottom
- **Severe symptoms** (seizure, coma, vomiting, confusion) → **3% saline 100–150 mL IV over 10–20 min**, repeat up to 2–3× → aim **+4–6 mEq/L** in a few hours.
- **Correction limit:** **≤8 mEq/L per 24 h** in high-risk patients (Na ≤105, alcohol use, malnutrition, hypokalemia, liver disease); ≤10–12 in others.
- **Check Na every 2–4 h** during active correction. Watch **urine output** — a big water diuresis = rapid rise.
- **Overcorrecting?** → **D5W + desmopressin 2 mcg IV** to re-lower.
- Work out the cause: **serum osm → urine osm → urine Na → volume status**.
:::

## 🧠 Pathophysiology in 60 seconds

- Hyponatremia is almost always a **water** problem: too much **ADH** or too little solute intake.
- Low serum Na → water moves **into brain cells** → cerebral edema (acute) → seizures.
- After **48 h** the brain adapts by losing osmolytes. Fast correction then pulls water **out** → **osmotic demyelination** (ODS): dysarthria, dysphagia, quadriparesis, "locked-in" — days later.

:::analogy
Think of the brain as a sponge that slowly squeezed itself smaller to fit. Refill the room too fast and the sponge tears.
:::

## 🔎 Diagnosis

{{fig:hypona-algo}}

- **Labs:** serum osm, urine osm, urine Na, glucose, BMP, TSH, AM cortisol, uric acid (low in SIADH), lipids/protein if pseudo.
- **SIADH criteria:** low serum osm, urine osm >100, urine Na >30, euvolemic, normal thyroid/adrenal/kidneys, no diuretics.
- **SIADH causes:** drugs (SSRIs, carbamazepine, thiazides, cyclophosphamide, MDMA), CNS disease, lung disease, small-cell cancer, pain, nausea, post-op.

## 💊 Management

:::tabs
=== Severe symptoms
- **3% saline 100–150 mL** over 10–20 min (or 2 mL/kg). Re-check Na. Repeat until symptoms improve (max ~3 boluses) or Na ↑ 4–6.
- Then stop and treat the cause.
- Rapid intermittent bolus (SALSA) ≈ continuous infusion for safety.
=== Mild–moderate
| Cause | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | **NS or LR**. Risk: when volume returns, ADH shuts off → **water diuresis** → overcorrection |
| SIADH | **Fluid restriction** <800–1000 mL/day; stop drug; **urea 15–30 g/day** or salt tablets + furosemide; tolvaptan (specialist; avoid in liver disease) |
| HF / cirrhosis | Fluid restriction, loop diuretic, treat disease; avoid hypertonic unless severe symptoms |
| Low solute (beer potomania, tea & toast) | Give solute slowly (food). **Very high ODS risk** |
| Thiazide | Stop thiazide, replace K |
| Adrenal insufficiency | **Hydrocortisone** (Na rises fast after) |
=== Overcorrection rescue
- Na rose >8 (high risk) or >10–12 in 24 h →
- **D5W** (e.g., 3 mL/kg/h) **+ desmopressin 2 mcg IV/SC q6–8h** to bring Na back down.
- Proactive "DDAVP clamp" for very high-risk patients (desmopressin + slow 3% saline).
:::

:::dose
Na change per 1 L of fluid (Adrogué-Madias) = (infusate Na + K − serum Na) ÷ (total body water + 1). Formulas often underestimate the rise — measure, do not trust the math.
:::

## ⚠️ Pitfalls

- NS in SIADH (urine osm higher than saline osm → Na can **fall**).
- Forgetting the K in IV fluids also raises Na.
- Leaving a patient on maintenance D5W with Na 124.
- Correcting too fast after a big water diuresis (hypovolemia, beer potomania, thiazide stop).

## 🎓 Board corner

| Stem | Answer |
|---|---|
| Na 118, seizure | 3% saline bolus |
| Euvolemic, U-osm 450, U-Na 50, lung mass | SIADH (small-cell) → fluid restriction, treat cause |
| Na 104, alcoholic, rose to 118 in 24 h | Overcorrection → D5W + DDAVP |
| Days after correction: dysarthria, quadriparesis | Osmotic demyelination |
| U-osm 60, drinks 10 L/day | Primary polydipsia |
| Na 128, glucose 900 | Translocational (corrected Na normal) |
