# Atrial fibrillation

<span class="reviewed">Last reviewed: 2026-11-18 — DRAFT, verify before use</span>

!!! tip "Bottom line"
    - Unstable (hypotension, shock, ischemia, acute HF from the rhythm)? **Synchronized cardioversion.**
    - Stable? Control the rate, find the trigger, and decide on anticoagulation.

??? info "Diagnosis"
    - ECG: irregularly irregular rhythm, no distinct P waves.
    - First workup: TSH, electrolytes (K, Mg), CBC, renal and liver function, echo.
    - 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS guideline uses **stages 1–4** (at risk → pre-AF → AF → permanent AF).

??? abstract "Quick pathophysiology"
    The atria lose one "conductor." Many small wavefronts compete, so the atria quiver. The AV node acts as a gate: it lets only some impulses through, so the ventricles beat fast and irregularly.

??? success "Management"
    **Rate control (stable)**

    - Beta-blocker or non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker.
    - Avoid diltiazem/verapamil in HFrEF.

    **Anticoagulation**

    - Use stroke-risk scoring (CHA₂DS₂-VASc) and clinical judgment.
    - Prefer a DOAC over warfarin, **except** with a mechanical valve or moderate–severe mitral stenosis (use warfarin).

    **Rhythm control**

    - Consider early rhythm control, especially in HFrEF. Catheter ablation is first-line for selected patients.

??? question "Board corner"
    - Pre-excited AF (WPW): **avoid AV-nodal blockers.** Use procainamide or cardioversion.
    - AF ≥ 48 h or unknown duration: anticoagulate ≥ 3 weeks or do TEE before cardioversion, then anticoagulate ≥ 4 weeks after.
